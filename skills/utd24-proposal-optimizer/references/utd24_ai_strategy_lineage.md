# AI × Strategy / Innovation / Entrepreneurship — UTD24 emerging lineages

This file extends `utd24_strategy_innovation_entrepreneurship_lineages.md` with the **AI / digital / algorithmic** lineages that have emerged at UTD24 outlets, especially since 2018. These lineages are still consolidating, which means:

1. **Conceptual definitions are contested** — what counts as "AI" varies across papers. Force the user to specify (rule-based / ML / deep learning / LLM / agentic).
2. **Anchor papers shift quickly** — what counts as central in 2024 may be peripheral in 2026. Recent-anchor citation is more important here than in mature lineages.
3. **Fashion-chase risk is high** — UTD24 reviewers are increasingly skeptical of "AI as marketing hook" papers. The mechanism must be specifically AI-driven, not generic-technology-driven.

Use this file when the user's phenomenon involves AI, ML, algorithmic decision-making, digital transformation, platform competition with AI, or human-AI collaboration.

## G1. Human-AI Complementarity / Augmentation (Raisch-Krakowski, Choudhury)

- **Central question**: When do AI systems substitute for vs complement human (managerial / professional) judgment, and how do organizations design for productive complementarity?
- **Canonical**: Raisch & Krakowski 2021 *AMR* "Artificial intelligence and management: the automation–augmentation paradox"; Choudhury, Starr & Agarwal 2020 *SMJ* "Machine learning and human capital complementarities"; Lebovitz, Lifshitz-Assaf & Levina 2022 *Organization Science* "To engage or not to engage with AI for critical judgments".
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "human-AI complementarity" OR "automation augmentation" + outlet:AMR/SMJ/OS + year ≥ 2023]
- **Typical method**: field experiment with AI rollout; archival panel of AI adoption; qual case of human-AI hybrid teams.
- **Outlet fit**: AMR ✓✓ (conceptual) / SMJ ✓ / OS ✓✓ / AMJ ✓
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - Treating "AI" generically; reviewers expect specification of AI type and capability
  - Predicting only substitution OR only complementarity — Raisch-Krakowski paradox view expects both, with boundary conditions
  - Using "AI adoption" as binary; modern reviewers expect granularity (which tasks, at what depth)

## G2. Algorithmic Decision-Making / Algorithmic Agency (Lindebaum, Murray)

- **Central question**: When algorithms take or shape decisions, what changes about authority, accountability, sense-making, and legitimacy within and across organizations?
- **Canonical**: Lindebaum, Vesa & den Hond 2020 *AMR* "Insights from "the machine stops" to better understand rational assumptions in algorithmic decision-making"; Murray, Rhymer & Sirmon 2021 *AMR* "Humans and technology: forms of conjoined agency in organizations"; Kellogg, Valentine & Christin 2020 *Academy of Management Annals* "Algorithms at work: the new contested terrain of control".
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "algorithmic decision" OR "algorithmic control" + organization + outlet:AMR/OS/ASQ/AMJ + year ≥ 2023]
- **Typical method**: qual case studies of algorithmic deployment; conceptual / theoretical pieces (AMR-dominant); occasionally vignette experiments.
- **Outlet fit**: AMR ✓✓ / OS ✓✓ / ASQ ✓ / AMJ ✓
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - Treating algorithmic decision-making as a generic "technology adoption" issue
  - Not engaging the human-judgment-displacement debate (when algos replace humans, what is lost — Lebovitz et al. 2022; Lindebaum-related)
  - Confusing automation, ML predictions, and agentic AI — they raise different theoretical questions

## G3. Cognitive Strategy in AI-Augmented Contexts (Csaszar, Gavetti extension)

- **Central question**: How does AI-augmented cognition change strategic decision-making at managerial and TMT levels, given that AI systems may expand, narrow, or distort the considered option set?
- **Canonical**: Csaszar 2018 *SMJ* "What makes a decision strategic?"; Csaszar & Steinberger 2022 *AMJ* "Organizations as artificial intelligences: the use of artificial intelligence analogies in organization theory"; Csaszar, Ketkar & Kim 2024 *Strategy Science*.
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "cognitive strategy" AND ("AI" OR "algorithmic") + outlet:SMJ/AMJ/SS + year ≥ 2023]
- **Typical method**: lab experiments with executives / MBAs; computational simulation; archival measurement of strategic option breadth via text analysis.
- **Outlet fit**: Strategy Science ✓✓ / OS ✓ / SMJ ✓ / AMJ ✓
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - Confusing managerial cognition with collective firm cognition
  - Not specifying whether AI augments deliberation vs replaces it (these have different cognitive implications)

## G4. TMT AI Literacy / Top-Management AI Engagement

- **Central question**: How do TMT characteristics related to AI (literacy, exposure, expertise, attitude) shape firm-level AI strategy and outcomes — extending the upper-echelons framework into the AI era?
- **Canonical**: This sub-lineage is emerging within the Hambrick-Mason upper-echelons tradition (Hambrick & Mason 1984 *AMR*; Hambrick 2007 *AMR*); recent anchors include Karunakaran, Orlikowski & Scott 2022 *OS* on professional engagement with algorithmic tools; AI-TMT-specific papers are emerging — search for them.
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "TMT AI" OR "CEO AI literacy" OR "top management team artificial intelligence" + outlet:SMJ/AMJ + year ≥ 2023]
- **Typical method**: archival panel with TMT-AI-experience measures (LinkedIn / biography coding); survey of executives; vignette experiments.
- **Outlet fit**: SMJ ✓ / AMJ ✓ / OS ✓
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - "AI literacy" measured as a binary or single-item; multi-dimensional construct needed
  - Not engaging Hambrick's (2007) critique of demographic-as-cognition proxies — TMT AI literacy is meant to be a more direct cognitive measure; defend the operationalization
  - The user's "ai-tmt-myopia" project (per project memory) sits here

## G5. Digital Strategy / Digital Transformation (Vial, Verhoef)

- **Central question**: How do firms strategize and reorganize when digital technologies (AI as one part) reshape value creation, capture, and competition?
- **Canonical**: Vial 2019 *Journal of Strategic Information Systems* "Understanding digital transformation: a review and a research agenda"; Verhoef et al. 2021 *J Business Research* (FT50 not UTD24); Hanelt et al. 2021 *J Management Studies* on digital transformation in incumbent organizations.
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "digital strategy" OR "digital transformation" + outlet:SMJ/AMJ/MS + year ≥ 2022]
- **Typical method**: archival panel; survey of executives; qual case of incumbent transformations.
- **Outlet fit**: SMJ ✓ / AMJ ✓ / MS ✓; Information Systems outlets (MISQ, ISR) for tech-focused angles
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - "Digital transformation" as a vague organizational-change concept; UTD24 reviewers expect specification of what specifically transforms (strategy, structure, processes, value proposition, value capture)
  - Conflating digital transformation with AI adoption — overlap but distinct

## G6. Platform Strategy in AI Era (Cusumano, Gawer-Cusumano)

- **Central question**: How do platforms compete and govern when AI shapes matching, recommendation, content moderation, and value creation across sides?
- **Canonical**: Cusumano, Gawer & Yoffie 2019 *The Business of Platforms*; Jacobides, Cennamo & Gawer 2018 *SMJ* "Towards a theory of ecosystems"; Hagiu & Wright 2015 *MS* "Multi-sided platforms".
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "platform" AND ("algorithm" OR "AI") + outlet:SMJ/MS + year ≥ 2022]
- **Typical method**: archival data on platform interactions; experiments on platform users / providers; formal modeling.
- **Outlet fit**: MS ✓✓ / SMJ ✓ / Strategy Science ✓
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - Not engaging the ecosystem framing (Adner, Jacobides, Cennamo-Gawer) for multi-sided platforms
  - Treating AI recommendation as the platform's "value proposition" without theorizing matching mechanism

## G7. AI in Innovation / R&D (Cockburn, Agrawal)

- **Central question**: How does AI as a research tool / "method of invention" reshape the innovation production function — search breadth, discovery rate, generality of innovations?
- **Canonical**: Cockburn, Henderson & Stern 2018 *NBER WP / chapter in Economics of AI*; Agrawal, Gans & Goldfarb 2019 *Strategy Science* "Exploring the impact of artificial intelligence: prediction versus judgment"; Bessen 2019 (book).
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "AI innovation" OR "machine learning R&D" + outlet:SMJ/MS/SS + year ≥ 2022]
- **Typical method**: archival patent / publication data; archival firm-level R&D investment data; occasional experiments.
- **Outlet fit**: MS ✓✓ / SMJ ✓ / Strategy Science ✓✓
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - Treating AI as an undifferentiated R&D input; specify which AI capability (prediction / pattern recognition / generation / decision support)
  - Not engaging the prediction-vs-judgment framing (Agrawal-Gans-Goldfarb)

## Fashion-chase prevention checklist (specific to AI papers)

When the user proposes an AI-themed paper, force the following before accepting it as UTD24-viable:

1. **Mechanism specificity**: is the theoretical mechanism specifically AI-driven, or could the same mechanism arise from any technology adoption? If the latter, the paper is fashion-chasing — rewrite the mechanism to use AI's specific features (prediction error, training-data dependence, opacity, generativity, agency).
2. **AI type specification**: does the paper name which AI capability is theoretically active (prediction / pattern recognition / generation / decision support / agentic)?
3. **Counterfactual technology**: would the paper's theoretical predictions differ if AI were replaced with rule-based automation? If no → paper is not AI-specific, reposition as digital transformation.
4. **Existing literature engagement**: does the paper engage at least 2 recent (≤ 5 years) UTD24-outlet papers from G1-G8? If no → the paper is positioning in an isolated way and reviewer will reject.
5. **Hot-topic anchoring**: does the paper's why-now reference a specific AI capability development (e.g., post-2022 generative AI, post-2023 enterprise LLM deployment) rather than a generic "AI is increasingly important"?

Papers that pass all 5 checks are AI-substantive. Papers that fail any 2 are fashion-chase risks; flag as DESK-REJECT-LEVEL risk per Dim 1.1 (fashion chase) in `utd24_rq_criteria.md`.
