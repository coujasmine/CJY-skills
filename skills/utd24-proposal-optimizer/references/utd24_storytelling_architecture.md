# Academic Storytelling Architecture — UTD24 coherence gate

Use this reference when the user asks whether a paper has a clear story, when
the draft feels like “everything is there but the paper is not coherent,” or
when IDEA, MANUSCRIPT, or REVIEW mode needs to test whether all five rubric
dimensions serve one theoretical problem.

This is a **cross-dimensional coherence gate**, not a sixth scored dimension.
The five-dimension total remains unchanged. Storytelling evidence should be
reported under the dimensions it diagnoses:

- Knot / important unresolved problem → Dim 1 (Research Question)
- Parent-theory tension → Dim 2 (Literature Conversation)
- Explanatory resolution → Dim 3 (Theoretical Mechanism)
- Predictions that advance one resolution → Dim 4 (Hypothesis Architecture)
- Evidence capable of resolving the problem → Dim 5 (Methods & Identification)

## 1. Storytelling definition and non-goals

Academic storytelling is **theoretical organization around one consequential
unresolved problem**. It is not novelistic prose, dramatic language, a catchy
opening anecdote, or packaging an ordinary variable relationship as important.

A coherent paper makes the reader experience four linked moves:

1. **Confusion:** existing knowledge cannot fully explain an important fact.
2. **Escalation:** the theory section shows why the problem is harder and more
   consequential than it initially appeared.
3. **Resolution:** the mechanism, design, and evidence provide a credible answer.
4. **Changed understanding:** the discussion states how the field must now think
   differently about the original problem.

Do not manufacture drama. Never invent a contradiction, suppress inconvenient
results, hide uncertainty, or overstate identification to make the story cleaner.
Coherence is subordinate to evidence. A null or unexpected finding belongs in
the story when it changes the theoretical answer; otherwise report it accurately
without falsely making it the centerpiece.

## 2. Four-move story spine

Before scoring or rewriting, try to complete this spine using only user-supplied
claims and evidence:

> Although the field generally understands **[phenomenon]** through
> **[existing explanation]**, **[anomaly / conflict / boundary condition]**
> remains unexplained. We propose **[mechanism]** and use **[design / evidence]**
> to show **[answer]**. Therefore, the field should revise **[old understanding]**
> into **[new understanding]**.

If one or more cells cannot be filled, label the missing cell rather than
inventing it:

- `[KNOT UNCLEAR]`
- `[PARENT-THEORY TENSION UNCLEAR]`
- `[MECHANISM UNCLEAR]`
- `[DESIGN CANNOT RESOLVE CLAIM]`
- `[ANSWER NOT SUPPORTED BY SUPPLIED RESULTS]`
- `[BEFORE-AFTER CONTRIBUTION UNCLEAR]`

The story spine is a diagnostic compression test. It is not automatically the
paper’s abstract and should not replace nuance or boundary conditions.

## 3. Gap-to-knot diagnostic

### Gap is not knot

A literature gap says that a relationship, context, construct, or population
has not been studied. It can establish novelty but rarely establishes theoretical
stakes by itself.

A knot identifies something that current knowledge cannot jointly explain. It
creates a reason the answer matters even if another paper has examined similar
variables.

### Three legitimate knot patterns

1. **Anomaly** — a consequential phenomenon behaves differently from what the
   parent theory predicts.
2. **Competing logics** — two credible theories or mechanisms imply different
   answers to the same question, and existing knowledge cannot specify which
   dominates or when.
3. **Failed hidden assumption** — a mainstream explanation relies on an
   implicit premise that does not hold in the focal setting.

### Gap-only warning signals

- “Few studies have examined …”
- “Little is known about …”
- “No research has tested A and B in context C …”
- “Prior work calls for more research …”
- “We add a moderator because prior studies have not considered it …”

These phrases are not forbidden. Flag them only when they carry the full burden
of importance and no anomaly, competing logic, or failed assumption follows.

### Gap-to-knot rewrite procedure

1. Remove the “few studies / little is known” sentence temporarily.
2. State what the parent theory would lead a reader to expect.
3. Identify the important observation, competing prediction, or setting feature
   that the expectation cannot explain.
4. Explain the cost of leaving the contradiction unresolved: which prediction,
   construct relationship, boundary, or strategic decision remains mistaken?
5. Formulate an RQ that asks for the explanation, not merely the missing test.

If importance disappears after step 1, the project currently has a gap but not
a knot. Report this under Dim 1 and Dim 2; do not fabricate a knot.

## 4. Theory as rising explanatory tension

The theory section should not function as separate legitimacy briefs for H1,
H2, H3, and H4. It should advance one explanatory chain:

1. **Existing explanation:** what the named parent theory currently explains.
2. **Hidden assumption:** what must be true for that explanation to work.
3. **Breakdown:** why the focal phenomenon or setting violates that assumption.
4. **New mechanism:** the directional process that resolves the problem.
5. **Distinctive prediction:** what should be observed if the new mechanism is
   correct rather than the strongest alternative.

### Paragraph-level tension test

For each theory subsection, identify:

- **Claim:** the theoretical judgment advanced here.
- **Basis:** the supplied theory or evidence that licenses the judgment.
- **Tension:** what existing understanding it complicates or corrects.
- **Advance:** how it moves the reader toward the mechanism or prediction.

A paragraph that has basis but no tension or advance may be literature summary
rather than theory development. Do not delete it automatically; decide whether
it is needed as context, can be compressed, or should move outside the main
argument.

### Multiple-story warning

Flag likely story proliferation when:

- one moderator requires an unrelated parent theory and several pages of setup;
- deleting H4 leaves H1-H3 and the contribution unchanged;
- each hypothesis answers a different RQ;
- an interesting result produces a second contribution unrelated to the knot;
- the title, abstract, introduction promise, and discussion contribution name
  different focal problems.

## 5. Methods and results as resolution

### Methods: why this design can answer

Once tension is established, the reader’s next question is whether the study can
actually resolve it. Methods should make four alignments explicit:

- **Setting alignment:** why the context reveals the proposed tension or mechanism.
- **Construct alignment:** why measures, manipulations, or codes represent the
  theoretical constructs rather than convenient proxies.
- **Identification alignment:** why the design distinguishes the proposed answer
  from reverse causality, selection, omitted variables, or alternative mechanisms.
- **Claim alignment:** which verbs and contribution claims the design licenses.

Storytelling never relaxes Dim 5. A rhetorically satisfying answer unsupported
by identification is a failed resolution.

### Results: organize by explanatory task

Do not treat table order as the narrative unit. Map each major analysis to one
of these functions:

| Analysis | Story function |
|---|---|
| Core test | Supplies the first answer to the RQ |
| Mechanism evidence | Explains why the answer arises |
| Alternative-explanation test | Rejects the strongest competing story |
| Boundary / heterogeneity analysis | Shows when the mechanism strengthens, weakens, or changes |
| Unexpected finding | Revises the theoretical answer when it materially changes understanding |

If an analysis has no explanatory task, keep it accurate but consider whether it
belongs in an appendix, robustness section, future paper, or the main story.

## 6. Discussion as before-after change

The discussion should answer: **because this study exists, what must the field
now understand differently about the original knot?**

Use this structure:

1. **Before:** the field’s prior default explanation or assumption.
2. **Deficiency:** what that view omitted, conflated, or treated as compatible.
3. **After:** the new mechanism-based understanding supported by the study.
4. **Consequences:** predictions, boundaries, constructs, or research choices that
   should change if the new understanding is accepted.

Weak contribution labels such as “extends the literature,” “fills a gap,” or
“responds to calls” are not prohibited, but they do not substitute for the
before-after proposition.

Claim strength must remain calibrated:

- Cross-sectional association → “suggests / is consistent with / indicates”
- Strong identification → causal language only to the extent licensed by Dim 5
- Qualitative process evidence → mechanism/process claims bounded by case logic
- Theory paper → logical change supported by internally consistent derivation

## 7. Story Coherence Gate

Run this gate after the five-dimension diagnosis, then map every failure back to
its affected dimensions. Do not alter the total score mechanically.

| Gate | PASS | PARTIAL | FAIL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knot | Important unresolved contradiction is explicit | Stakes are plausible but gap still carries importance | Only “not studied” or variable pairing |
| Tension escalation | Theory progressively exposes limits and motivates one mechanism | Some progression but hypotheses remain partly modular | Theory is a stack of independent justifications |
| Resolution | Method and results directly answer the knot and engage alternatives | Core answer exists but mechanism or identification is incomplete | Evidence cannot answer the promised question |
| Before-after | Discussion states a specific change in theoretical understanding | Contribution is identifiable but generic or weakly bounded | Only topic labels, gap-fill, or repeated results |
| One-story coherence | Title through discussion serve one RQ and mechanism | One secondary line competes with main story | Multiple separable papers stitched together |

### Overall gate result

- **COHERENT:** all five gates PASS, or four PASS + one defensible PARTIAL.
- **REPAIRABLE:** no Resolution FAIL, but one or more other gates are PARTIAL/FAIL.
- **STRUCTURAL REBUILD:** Resolution FAIL, or Knot + One-story coherence both FAIL.

The gate result is reported separately from `/100`. A manuscript can score well
on individual dimensions yet remain incoherent; conversely, a coherent story
cannot rescue weak identification or fabricated positioning.

## 8. Mode-specific workflows

### IDEA mode

1. Complete the four-move story spine.
2. Classify the knot as anomaly, competing logics, failed assumption, or gap-only.
3. Generate RQ variants that preserve the same phenomenon but change the
   theoretical knot; do not generate cosmetic wording variants.
4. State the proposed before-after contribution at LOW confidence.
5. Map missing cells to P0 actions.

Required storytelling output:

- Knot classification
- One-sentence story spine
- Gap-to-knot rewrite if needed
- Provisional before-after contribution

### MANUSCRIPT mode

1. Extract the promise from title, abstract, and introduction.
2. Map theory subsections to existing explanation → hidden assumption → breakdown
   → new mechanism → predictions.
3. Map methods and analyses to their resolution tasks.
4. Extract the discussion’s before-after proposition.
5. Run the Story Coherence Gate and recommend targeted pruning.

Required storytelling output:

- Story spine using the user’s current text
- Section-to-story map
- Gate table with evidence locations
- “Keep / Compress / Move / Remove (with reason)” decisions

### REVIEW mode

Run the gate adversarially. Reviewer 1 attacks theory escalation and mechanism;
Reviewer 2 attacks whether the resolution is empirically licensed; Reviewer 3
attacks knot importance and before-after contribution; the AE determines whether
the incoherence is repairable or indicates multiple stitched papers.

Required storytelling output:

- Strongest “what is this paper really about?” objection
- Gate result and most damaging failure
- One repair path that preserves evidence
- Structural-rebuild warning if the current design cannot resolve the promised knot

### DESIGN mode

Do not run a full storytelling audit by default. Use the story spine only to
verify that the proposed design targets the same knot and mechanism established
in IDEA. If the design answers a different question, return to IDEA framing
before selecting methods.

## 9. Pruning and revision rules

For each major element, assign one action:

- **KEEP:** directly establishes the knot, escalates tension, supplies resolution,
  or states the changed understanding.
- **COMPRESS:** necessary context but not a distinct theoretical move.
- **MOVE:** accurate and useful, but belongs in background, robustness, appendix,
  limitations, or a future paper.
- **REMOVE:** redundant or unsupported and cannot be relocated. Under Hard Rule 8,
  list it under “Removed (with reason).”

Never remove evidence merely because it complicates the preferred story. When
evidence contradicts the proposed mechanism, revise the answer, reduce claim
strength, or mark the project as needing theoretical rebuild.

### Fast pruning questions

- If this hypothesis disappears, does the core answer change?
- Does this moderator strengthen the knot or create a second paper?
- Does this result answer, explain, bound, or challenge the RQ?
- Does this literature paragraph perform a theoretical move or only display coverage?
- Can the claimed contribution be stated without “first,” “gap,” or “call for research”?


```markdown

| Resolution | PASS/PARTIAL/FAIL | ... | ... |
| Before-after | PASS/PARTIAL/FAIL | ... | ... |
| One-story coherence | PASS/PARTIAL/FAIL | ... | ... |

Overall: COHERENT / REPAIRABLE / STRUCTURAL REBUILD

```

### Section-to-story map

```markdown
| Section / element | Story task | Current status | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Establish knot | ... | KEEP/COMPRESS/MOVE/REMOVE |
| Theory | Escalate tension + introduce mechanism | ... | ... |
| Methods | Establish ability to resolve | ... | ... |
| Results | Answer / explain / rule out / bound | ... | ... |
| Discussion | State before-after change | ... | ... |
```
