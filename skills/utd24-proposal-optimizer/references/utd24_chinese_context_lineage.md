# Chinese Context / Emerging Market Lineages — UTD24 parent theories

This file extends the parent-theory lineages in `utd24_strategy_innovation_entrepreneurship_lineages.md` with **Chinese-context and emerging-market lineages**. Chinese-context papers are common at UTD24 strategy / innovation / entrepreneurship outlets (especially SMJ, AMJ, MS-Strategy section), and the reviewer pool increasingly expects the paper to engage these lineages explicitly when the data is from China or emerging markets — not just to use Chinese data within a Western-theory frame.

UTD24 reviewers in this space distinguish between:

- **Context-as-setting** (paper uses Chinese data to test a Western theory) — acceptable but increasingly criticized for not contributing to context-specific theory
- **Context-as-theory** (paper extends or develops theory grounded in Chinese institutional features) — strongly favored at UTD24 in the last 5 years

This file's lineages support the second mode.

For lineages outside this file (Western mainstream), see `utd24_strategy_innovation_entrepreneurship_lineages.md`.

## F1. Institutional Voids / Emerging Market Institutions (Khanna-Palepu)

- **Central question**: How do firms in emerging markets compensate for missing or weak intermediary institutions (capital markets, labor markets, product-market regulators, legal enforcement)?
- **Canonical**: Khanna & Palepu 1997 *HBR* "Why focused strategies may be wrong for emerging markets"; Khanna & Palepu 2000 *JF* "Is group affiliation profitable in emerging markets?"; Khanna, Palepu & Sinha 2005 *HBR* "Strategies that fit emerging markets"; Hoskisson, Eden, Lau & Wright 2000 *AMJ* (institutional context typology).
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "institutional voids" OR "Khanna Palepu" + outlet:SMJ/AMJ/MS + year ≥ 2020]
- **Typical method**: archival panel of business groups, M&A activity, IPOs; instrumental-variable strategies using institutional shocks (e.g., regulatory reforms).
- **Outlet fit**: SMJ ✓✓ / AMJ ✓ / MS ✓ / OS ✓ / ASQ △
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - "Emerging market" used as a label without specifying which institutional voids matter → reviewer reject
  - Treating China as a generic "emerging market" without engaging China-specific institutional features (party-state, hukou, SOE governance) → looks shallow
  - Citing Khanna-Palepu without engaging the recent critique that "institutional voids" framing pathologizes context (Chari et al. 2021 *JIBS* among others)

## F2. State-Firm Relations / Political Connections (Faccio, Peng, Sun)

- **Central question**: How do firms' connections to and embeddedness in the state apparatus shape strategy and performance?
- **Canonical**: Faccio 2006 *AER* "Politically connected firms"; Peng 2003 *AMR* "Institutional transitions and strategic choices"; Sun, Mellahi & Wright 2012 *Long Range Planning* (China-focused review); Marquis & Qian 2014 *OS* "Corporate social responsibility reporting in China"; Wang, Du & Marquis 2019 *AMJ* (state-firm relations).
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "political ties" OR "state ownership" + China + outlet:SMJ/AMJ + year ≥ 2020]
- **Typical method**: archival panel of state-owned vs private firms; political-connection measurement via directors / executives' political backgrounds; difference-in-differences around political-cycle shocks (e.g., anti-corruption campaign, leadership transitions).
- **Outlet fit**: SMJ ✓✓ / AMJ ✓✓ / OS ✓ / MS ✓
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - Treating "state ownership" and "political connection" as the same construct — they are distinct
  - Using political-connection measures without engaging the Faccio mechanism (rent extraction vs information access vs legitimacy)
  - Not engaging the 2013-onwards anti-corruption shocks (Xi-era) as natural experiments — UTD24 reviewers in this space expect this engagement

## F3. Guanxi and Relational Strategy (Park-Luo, Xin-Pearce)

- **Central question**: How do interpersonal relationships (guanxi) shape inter-firm cooperation, contracting, and performance in China?
- **Canonical**: Xin & Pearce 1996 *AMJ* "Guanxi: connections as substitutes for formal institutional support"; Park & Luo 2001 *SMJ* "Guanxi and organizational dynamics"; Luo, Huang & Wang 2012 *JIBS* (guanxi review); Chen, Chen & Huang 2013 *MOR* (guanxi multi-level framework).
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "guanxi" OR "social capital China" + outlet:AMJ/SMJ/MOR + year ≥ 2020]
- **Typical method**: survey of Chinese executives / managers; archival deal-level data; ethnographic case (rare but ASQ-favored).
- **Outlet fit**: AMJ ✓✓ / SMJ ✓ / MOR ✓✓ (FT50 but not UTD24-strict) / ASQ ✓
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - Treating guanxi as undifferentiated "social capital" without distinguishing types (familial, classmate, business, official)
  - Assuming guanxi is positive without engaging its dark side (cronyism, rent-seeking, suppression of meritocracy — Luo 2008 *JIBS*)
  - Reviewer skepticism is highest when measuring guanxi via single-item self-report — multi-item scales (Chen et al. 2013) or behavioral indicators preferred

## F4. Family Business and Chinese-Family-Firm Governance (Carney, Bertrand)

- **Central question**: How does family ownership and control shape strategic decisions, succession, and performance — and how do these dynamics differ in Chinese family firms?
- **Canonical**: Bertrand & Schoar 2006 *JEP* "The role of family in family firms"; Carney 2005 *ETP* "Corporate governance and competitive advantage in family-controlled firms"; Anderson & Reeb 2003 *JF*; Yu, Lumpkin, Sorenson & Brigham 2012 *FBR* (Chinese family business review); Zheng, Wei & Zhang 2020 *AMJ* (family firm succession in China).
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "family firm" + China + outlet:SMJ/AMJ + year ≥ 2020]
- **Typical method**: archival panel with family-ownership indicators; succession event studies; survey of family-firm executives.
- **Outlet fit**: AMJ ✓✓ / SMJ ✓ / ASQ ✓; *Family Business Review* (FT50-adjacent, not UTD24).
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - Treating Chinese family firms as identical to Western family firms — succession patterns, intergenerational relationships, and state interactions differ
  - Not engaging socioemotional-wealth framework (Gomez-Mejia et al. 2007 AMR onward) when relevant
  - Using "family ownership %" as the sole proxy without considering control (voting rights, board chair, CEO position)

## F5. Chinese Institutional Transitions / Reform Periods (Peng)

- **Central question**: How do firms respond to discrete institutional transitions (reform-era China being the paradigmatic case) and what strategic capabilities emerge in transitional contexts?
- **Canonical**: Peng & Heath 1996 *AMR* "The growth of the firm in planned economies in transition"; Peng 2003 *AMR* "Institutional transitions and strategic choices"; Peng 2002 *APJM* "Towards an institution-based view of business strategy".
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "institutional transition" OR "institution-based view" + China + outlet:SMJ/AMJ/MS + year ≥ 2020]
- **Typical method**: panel data spanning reform-period shocks; DiD around regulatory transitions; cross-region variation in reform timing.
- **Outlet fit**: SMJ ✓ / AMJ ✓ / MS ✓ / APJM ✓✓ (FT50, not UTD24-strict)
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - "Institution-based view" used as label without specifying which institutional dimension (regulatory / normative / cognitive) is theoretically active
  - Not addressing whether the "transition" is still ongoing or has stabilized — affects the theoretical lens

## F6. Network Capitalism and Chinese Business Groups (Boisot-Child, Keister)

## F8. Confucian Cultural Values and Management (Hofstede, Schwartz extended; China-specific)

- **Central question**: How do Confucian values (collectivism, hierarchy, harmony, long-term orientation, face) shape managerial behavior and firm-level strategy?
- **Canonical**: Hofstede 1980/2001 *Culture's Consequences*; Bond 1988 (Chinese Values Survey); House et al. 2004 *GLOBE*; Tsui, Wang & Xin 2006 *MOR*.
- **Recent UTD24 in lineage**: [CITATION NEEDED — user supplies; suggested search query: "Confucian" OR "Chinese cultural values" + management + outlet:AMJ/ASQ/MOR + year ≥ 2020]
- **Typical method**: cross-cultural survey; archival comparison across cultural contexts; experiment with cultural-prime manipulation.
- **Outlet fit**: AMJ ✓ / MOR ✓✓ (FT50, not UTD24-strict) / ASQ ✓; rare at SMJ/MS
- **Mis-application warnings**:
  - Using Hofstede scores at country level for firm-level claims (ecological fallacy)
  - Treating "Chinese culture" as monolithic across the 1.4B population
  - Not engaging the within-China cultural variation (regional, generational, urban-rural)

## Integration patterns specific to Chinese context

UTD24 acceptance has been favorable for integrations like:

| Integration | Logic |
|---|---|
| **Institutional voids × Western theory** | E.g., RBV in institutional-voids context: which resources are valuable when intermediaries are missing? |
| **Political ties × upper echelons** | E.g., do politically-connected CEOs differ on cognition / experience effects vs non-connected? |
| **Guanxi × dynamic capabilities** | E.g., do guanxi-rich firms have different reconfiguration patterns? |
| **Confucian values × behavioral theory** | E.g., how does long-term orientation shape aspiration formation and search? |
| **State ownership × innovation** | E.g., do SOEs and private firms have systematically different innovation strategies? |

**Do not propose an integration the user did not already gesture at.** Suggest, do not impose.

## When to flag "Chinese context risk"

When the user's draft uses Chinese data but **does not** engage Chinese-context lineages:

- **MEDIUM risk** if the paper is testing a Western theory and Chinese context is incidental (acceptable but flag in DESIGN / MANUSCRIPT mode: "your reviewer pool will include scholars who expect engagement with Chinese-context literature; consider adding F1/F2/F3 anchors in the discussion section even if not in the main theory")
- **HIGH risk** if the paper makes phenomenon-specific claims about China without engaging Chinese-context theory (reviewers will reject as "context exoticism" — using China as a novelty hook without theorizing context)

Specifically for UTD24:
- Submitting a paper with China data to AMJ without engaging F1-F8 → R1 will likely flag
- Submitting to ASQ → expects deeper engagement, including potentially ethnographic / institutional analysis
- Submitting to SMJ → engagement with F1-F2 (institutional voids, state-firm relations) is the most common path
- Submitting to MOR (FT50 but not UTD24-strict) → engagement with F3-F8 is expected
