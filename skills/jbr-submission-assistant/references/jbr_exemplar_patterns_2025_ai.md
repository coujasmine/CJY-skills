# JBR Exemplar Patterns: 2025 AI and Digital Transformation Papers

Use this reference when the user asks to learn from recent JBR articles, calibrate an AI/digitalization manuscript, or strengthen a manuscript involving AI adoption, AI capabilities, employee/workplace outcomes, NPD, innovation, agility, or performance.

## Exemplar 1: AI Adoption and Employee Wellbeing

Core model:

- AI adoption does not directly improve employee wellbeing.
- AI adoption influences wellbeing indirectly through task optimization and workplace safety/cybersecurity.
- Job Demands-Resources theory supplies the mechanism: AI changes job resources and demands, and these work characteristics shape wellbeing.

Writing pattern:

- Open with digital technologies transforming work, then quickly move to employee-level concerns created by AI and Industry 5.0.
- Use concrete AI-in-workplace examples to make the phenomenon feel real.
- Avoid treating AI as automatically beneficial. Build an ambivalent setup: AI can improve work but can also create surveillance, bias, privacy, and stress concerns.
- Convert the broad AI debate into a mechanism gap: how AI affects wellbeing through concrete work-related factors remains unclear.
- State the research question explicitly: how AI adoption affects employee wellbeing.
- Report a null direct effect honestly, then use it to sharpen the contribution: AI benefits are not automatic but mediated by work redesign and safety.

Contribution pattern:

- Contribution 1: Shift AI adoption research from productivity/efficiency toward social and employee wellbeing outcomes.
- Contribution 2: Extend employee wellbeing research by linking wellbeing to AI-shaped work factors.
- Contribution 3: Add evidence on the mechanisms through which workplace AI affects employees.

Method pattern:

- Justify the empirical setting as theoretically relevant for AI adoption, not just convenient.
- Explain why the informant group can see both technology implementation and workforce consequences.
- Justify nonstandard measurement choices, such as single-item wellbeing or narrow two-item constructs.
- Use CFA/SEM, common method checks, and sample-bias discussion to protect credibility.

Transferable lesson:

- For AI manuscripts, JBR rewards mechanism discipline. A simple AI-to-outcome relationship is not enough; the paper should show the work process, capability, attention, decision, or safety pathway through which AI matters.

## Exemplar 2: Design Thinking, AI Capabilities, and NPD Agility

Core model:

- Design thinking and AI capabilities improve NPD decision-making agility.
- NPD decision-making agility channels these capabilities into NPD performance.
- Organizational formalization has opposite boundary effects: it weakens the design-thinking-to-agility link but strengthens the AI-capabilities-to-agility link.

Writing pattern:

- Open with a concrete business challenge: dynamic markets and short-lived competitive advantage make NPD decisions harder.
- Introduce two practically salient capabilities, one human-centered and one technology-driven.
- Use firm examples early to show relevance, then pivot to unresolved theory.
- Build two gaps: a mechanism gap about how capabilities become performance, and a boundary-condition gap about which organizational structures help or hurt.
- State research questions that mirror the model: through what mechanism, and under what structural conditions.
- Use theory pairing with clear roles: dynamic capabilities explains design thinking and capability value; structural alignment explains formalization as boundary condition.

Contribution pattern:

- Recast design thinking as an organizational-level dynamic capability, not only a toolbox, process, or mindset.
- Position AI capabilities as technology-driven innovation enablers.
- Treat agility as the mediating "funnel" that converts capabilities into performance.
- Use the opposite moderation finding to create theoretical bite: different capabilities require different structural alignments.

Method pattern:

- Define the sampling frame around the phenomenon: U.S. firms with recent NPD activity.
- Use senior, knowledgeable key informants and document screening criteria.
- Justify PLS-SEM when the model is prediction-oriented, complex, or includes formative constructs.
- Report procedural and statistical common-method remedies.
- Use simple-slope and floodlight/Johnson-Neyman analysis for moderation where useful.

Transferable lesson:

- JBR can accept a capability-performance model when the paper makes the conversion mechanism and the structural boundary conditions explicit. The contribution comes from explaining when a capability helps, not merely showing that it has a positive effect.

## Cross-Article JBR Lessons

Use these patterns when diagnosing or rewriting AI/digitalization manuscripts:

- Start from a live business problem, then narrow to an unresolved theoretical mechanism.
- Treat AI as a situated organizational capability or work-changing force, not as a generic technology label.
- Include both promise and risk in the setup; this creates a stronger puzzle than one-sided technological optimism.
- Make the mediator do real theoretical work. In these exemplars, task optimization, safety, and decision-making agility are not statistical decorations; they explain how AI/capabilities translate into outcomes.
- Use moderators to reveal fit or misfit between technology, people, structure, and context.
- Turn null or mixed results into insight when they refine the mechanism.
- Justify the empirical context as a place where the theory is especially observable.
- Justify informants carefully in survey studies, especially when the outcome concerns employees but respondents are managers.
- Keep implications implementation-specific: tell managers what to change in tasks, safety, structure, communication, training, or decision routines.
- Keep limitations tied to actual design constraints: cross-sectional data, single informants, country context, early-stage measures, and unmeasured negative effects.

## Application to AI, TMT Attention, and Managerial Myopia Manuscripts

When applying these lessons to an AI-TMT-attention or managerial short-termism paper:

- Do not frame the study as "AI affects managerial myopia" alone. Specify the attentional or decision mechanism through which AI changes temporal orientation.
- Make AI concrete: AI adoption, AI capability, AI attention, AI investment, AI governance, or AI-enabled decision systems should be conceptually distinct.
- Treat TMT attention as a mediator, moderator, or mechanism only if the paper can explain how attention is allocated, redirected, narrowed, or broadened.
- Build a boundary condition around organizational structure, environmental dynamism, governance, slack, incentive horizon, or digital capability when the theory needs contingency.
- If the empirical design is archival and correlational, use association-based language and avoid claiming that AI "causes" myopia unless identification supports it.
- Make the setting theoretically meaningful: explain why the sample, industry, period, or AI shock reveals the short-termism mechanism.
