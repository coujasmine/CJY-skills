# JBR Scope and Format

Source note: Based on Elsevier ScienceDirect's Journal of Business Research Guide for Authors checked on 2026-05-17. Verify the current official guide before final submission because journal pages and submission systems can change.

Official guide: https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-business-research/publish/guide-for-authors

## Scope Fit

JBR fit requires more than a management topic. The manuscript should speak to business research in contexts such as entrepreneurship, innovation management, strategy, organizational behavior, human resources, marketing, service research, international business, digital transformation, or related decision contexts.

Check whether the paper:

- Addresses a business decision, process, activity, market, organization, or stakeholder issue.
- Offers rigorous research with clear theory, credible evidence, and transparent methods.
- Is relevant to scholarship, practice, and/or society.
- Has potentially meaningful impact beyond a narrow sample novelty.
- Connects findings to business decisions, organizational action, managerial judgment, policy, or societal implications.

Weak fit signals:

- The paper is a generic social science paper with only incidental business implications.
- The contribution is only "this relationship has not been studied in this country/industry."
- The manuscript has interesting data but no theory-facing question.
- Practical implications are generic advice not grounded in the findings.

## Current Formatting Checks

Use these as submission QA checks, then re-check the official guide and submission portal:

- Initial full-length manuscript or review article: no more than 45 double-spaced pages, including figures, tables, and references.
- Abstract: no more than 150 words.
- Keywords: use 4-6 keywords for JBR article-type checks. Note that Elsevier's general keyword instruction on the same guide may state a wider range; flag this if the portal differs and follow the current portal.
- Final accepted manuscript: normally no longer than 8000 words all inclusive, with no appendices.
- Peer review: double-anonymized.
- Title page: separate author-identifying title page.
- Blinded manuscript: remove author names, affiliations, acknowledgments, funding clues, author notes, and file metadata that could identify authors.
- Tables, figures, and references: place at the end of the manuscript for the submission version when required by the guide.
- Declarations: synchronize funding, competing interests, data availability, author contributions, ethics/consent statements where applicable.

## Final QA Questions

- Does the title page match the manuscript title exactly?
- Does the blinded file reveal identity through acknowledgments, self-citations, institution names, IRB text, author biographies, file properties, or appendix headers?
- Do abstract, introduction, cover letter, and discussion describe the same contribution?
- Are keywords present and within the current required range?
- Are all tables and figures cited in order?
- Are references complete and consistent with in-text citations?
- Are supplementary files named and referenced consistently?

## Special Issue Fit

When the user targets a special issue:

- Ask for the call for papers or key text.
- Map the manuscript's research question, theory, method, and implications to the call's explicit themes.
- Do not claim fit based only on shared keywords.
- If fit is partial, recommend reframing the introduction and contribution around the call's central problem.
