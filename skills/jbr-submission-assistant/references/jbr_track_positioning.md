# JBR Track Positioning

Use this file when judging JBR fit or strengthening the manuscript's introduction, contribution, method rationale, or practical implications. The goal is to name the most plausible JBR disciplinary track and the review risk attached to that positioning.

## Track Matching Table

| Track | Suitable topics | Typical theory lenses | Common methods | Fit risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic Management | Firm capabilities, strategic change, temporal orientation, resilience, acquisitions, resource allocation, governance | RBV, dynamic capabilities, attention-based view, behavioral theory of the firm, upper echelons | Archival panel, DiD, IV, survey, mixed methods | Too macro; weak managerial mechanism; no clear strategic decision context |
| Big Data and Business Analytics | AI adoption, analytics capability, data infrastructure, algorithmic decision-making, digital transformation | Information processing, dynamic capabilities, sociotechnical systems, organizational learning | Survey, archival, text analytics, mixed methods, experiments | Too technical; AI framed as a tool rather than a business capability or managerial process |
| Innovation, Marketing, and Management | NPD, innovation capability, design thinking, digital innovation, customer/market response | Dynamic capabilities, KBV, innovation search, absorptive capacity | Survey, PLS-SEM/SEM, archival innovation data, panel, experiments | Contribution collapses into performance effect; innovation mechanism under-specified |
| Organizational Behavior and Human Resource Management | Employee wellbeing, leadership, identity, workplace AI, team processes, HR practices | JD-R, social exchange, social identity, upper echelons, attention-based view | Survey, multi-source survey, experiment, qualitative, mixed methods | Business relevance appears secondary to psychology; managerial implications too generic |
| International Business | MNEs, headquarters-subsidiary relations, institutional distance, cross-border management, global strategy | Institutional theory, transaction cost, agency, social exchange, identity | Dyadic survey, panel, qualitative, mixed methods | Country setting is convenient rather than theoretically informative |
| Entrepreneurship | Opportunity, venture scaling, entrepreneurial cognition, digital entrepreneurship, founder decisions | Opportunity development, bricolage, effectuation, dynamic capabilities | Survey, qualitative case, archival, experiment | Descriptive venture story without theoretical abstraction |
| Consumer Behavior and Marketing | Consumer decisions, digital marketing, brand, service, platforms, customer analytics | Consumer psychology, signaling, information processing, service-dominant logic | Experiment, survey, field data, platform data | Business decision maker unclear; managerial implication thin |

## How to Use

1. **Name the primary track** in the audit or review verdict.
2. **Name the secondary track** when the paper sits between two conversations, such as Strategic Management and Big Data and Business Analytics.
3. **Translate track choice into reviewer risk.** A Strategic Management framing invites theory and identification scrutiny; a Big Data and Business Analytics framing invites measurement and managerial-process scrutiny.
4. **Do not force a track.** If the manuscript lacks a business decision context or managerial relevance, report weak JBR fit.

## Track-Specific Contribution Moves

Use these contribution moves when rewriting contribution paragraphs:

- **Strategic Management**: specify how a capability, governance condition, or managerial attention pattern changes strategic behavior or resource allocation.
- **Big Data and Business Analytics**: specify how data/AI capability becomes a business process, decision routine, or organizational capability.
- **Innovation, Marketing, and Management**: specify the conversion mechanism from capability to innovation outcome, and the boundary condition under which conversion strengthens or weakens.
- **OB/HRM**: specify the work-system or managerial mechanism connecting the focal practice to employee or team outcomes, and translate it back to business performance or organizational decision making.
- **International Business**: specify why cross-border, institutional, or HQ-subsidiary context changes the mechanism.
- **Entrepreneurship**: specify how the phenomenon changes opportunity recognition, resource orchestration, or venture scaling.
- **Consumer/Marketing**: specify the customer, channel, or market decision that gives the contribution business relevance.

## Fit Verdict Language

Use precise fit language:

- `Strong JBR fit through [track]`: the manuscript has a clear business phenomenon, theory conversation, and method suited to the track.
- `Conditional JBR fit through [track]`: fit is plausible, but the introduction/contribution/method rationale must be repositioned.
- `Weak JBR fit`: the topic is adjacent to business research but lacks a clear managerial decision context or theory contribution.
- `Poor JBR fit`: the paper is primarily technical, psychological, methodological, or policy-oriented without a business research conversation.

## AI and TMT Attention Example

For manuscripts on AI capability, TMT attention, and managerial myopia, first test three possible tracks:

- **Strategic Management** if the paper's core question is how AI capability changes top managers' temporal orientation, strategic attention allocation, and firm-level resource commitments.
- **Big Data and Business Analytics** if the paper's core question is how AI/data capability changes managerial information processing and decision routines.
- **Innovation, Marketing, and Management** if the paper's core question is how AI capability affects innovation search, exploration, NPD, or market-facing outcomes.

The safest JBR positioning is usually the track where the paper can name a business decision, a theory lens, and a method-supported mechanism in the first two introduction pages.
