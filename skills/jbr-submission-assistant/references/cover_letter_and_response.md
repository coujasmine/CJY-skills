# Cover Letter and Response Letter

Use this reference for JBR cover letters, reviewer responses, and submission-package language.

## Cover Letter Logic

A cover letter should be concise and aligned with the manuscript. It should not over-sell the paper or introduce claims absent from the manuscript.

Recommended structure:

1. Manuscript title and article type.
2. One-sentence business problem or theoretical puzzle.
3. Study design in one sentence.
4. Main contribution to theory.
5. Practical and/or social relevance.
6. Fit with JBR or special issue.
7. Required statements: originality, not under consideration elsewhere, author approval, conflicts/funding/data availability where appropriate.

Avoid:

- "This is the first paper" unless the manuscript substantiates it carefully.
- Claims broader than the method supports.
- Long literature reviews.
- Repeating the abstract.

## Cover Letter Template

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, "[TITLE]," for publication in the Journal of Business Research.

The manuscript examines [BUSINESS PROBLEM OR THEORETICAL TENSION]. Drawing on [THEORY/LENS], we ask [RESEARCH QUESTION]. Using [DATA/CONTEXT/METHOD], we find that [CALIBRATED MAIN FINDING].

The study contributes to [LITERATURE] by [SPECIFIC CONTRIBUTION TYPE: mechanism, boundary condition, integration, clarification, reconciliation, or contextualization]. It also offers implications for [MANAGERS/POLICYMAKERS/STAKEHOLDERS] by [PRACTICAL IMPLICATION TIED TO FINDINGS].

We believe the manuscript fits JBR's interest in rigorous, relevant, and impactful business research because [FIT STATEMENT]. The manuscript is original, is not under review elsewhere, and all authors have approved its submission. [ADD DECLARATIONS AS REQUIRED.]

Sincerely,

[CORRESPONDING AUTHOR]

## Reviewer Response Principles

Use a respectful, evidence-led, non-defensive tone.

For each comment:

- Thank the reviewer briefly.
- State the action taken.
- Explain the reasoning if the change is partial or the suggestion is not adopted.
- Quote or summarize the exact manuscript change.
- Provide page/line references when available.

Response structure:

1. Opening note summarizing major changes.
2. Reviewer-by-reviewer responses.
3. Comment, response, manuscript change.
4. Closing note.

Good response verbs:

- Clarified, revised, added, removed, softened, reanalyzed, reported, relocated, expanded, streamlined.

Avoid:

- "We disagree" as the first move.
- Defensive explanations without manuscript changes.
- Adding large unsupported claims to satisfy a reviewer.
- Saying a limitation is solved when it is only acknowledged.

## Response Template

Comment [R1.1]: [Reviewer comment]

Response: Thank you for this helpful suggestion. We have [ACTION]. Specifically, we revised [SECTION] to [PURPOSE]. The revised text now states: "[SHORT EXCERPT OR PARAPHRASE]." This change appears on [PAGE/LINE].

If partial adoption:

Response: Thank you for raising this point. We agree that [VALID PART]. We have therefore [ACTION]. We did not [REQUESTED ACTION] because [METHOD/THEORY/SPACE REASON], but we now acknowledge this boundary by [ACTION].
