# JBR Submission Workflow

Use this sequence for manuscripts that already have a full or near-full draft and need to become submission-ready.

## Stage 1: Manuscript Audit

Objective: Decide whether the paper has a publishable core and identify the highest-leverage revision priorities.

Check:

- Clear research question with managerial and scholarly relevance.
- Introduction frames a real phenomenon or contradiction, not only a literature gap.
- Theoretical lens is coherent rather than stitched together.
- Hypotheses/propositions follow from theory.
- Empirical design tests the stated claims.
- Misalignments across introduction, theory, methods, results, and discussion.

Output:

- Fit/readiness judgment.
- Top three revision priorities.
- Fatal, major, moderate, and cosmetic issue list.

## Stage 2: Core Argument Alignment

Objective: Make one stable storyline connect the research question, theory, hypotheses, evidence, and claims.

Check:

- One-sentence core claim.
- The causal or explanatory chain the evidence can support.
- Research question aligned with theoretical problem.
- Hypotheses aligned with mechanism rather than data availability.
- Discussion claims aligned with actual evidence.
- Clear statement of what the paper is not claiming.

## Stage 3: Theory and Hypotheses

Objective: Make the manuscript read as a scholarly argument rather than a variable-based empirical exercise.

Check:

- One primary theoretical conversation.
- Auxiliary theories serve the main lens.
- Mechanism is explicit: what changes, through what process, and why.
- Moderators/mediators are necessary to the theory.
- Nonlinear or dual-path claims are conceptually justified before testing.

## Stage 4: Methods and Evidence

Objective: Ensure empirical design, measures, models, and robustness checks are sufficient for the claims.

Check:

- Construct-to-measure consistency.
- Level-of-analysis consistency.
- Identification logic and alternative explanations.
- Model choice and robustness design.
- Claim language calibrated to evidence strength.

## Stage 5: High-Leverage Rewriting

Objective: Rewrite title, abstract, introduction, and discussion after the argument and evidence are stable.

Check:

- Title is specific and credible.
- Abstract makes problem, theory, design, findings, and contribution understandable.
- Introduction follows phenomenon, tension, insufficiency, question, design, contribution.
- Discussion interprets findings and separates theory contribution, practical/social implications, limitations, and future research.

## Stage 6: Presentation Standardization

Objective: Produce a clean manuscript with consistent terminology and apparatus.

Check:

- Headings, terminology, construct names, hypothesis labels, model numbers, sample sizes.
- Table titles, notes, variable labels, significance notation.
- Figure titles and captions.
- Citation/reference match.
- Removed drafting notes, duplicate text, and tracked-change residue.

## Stage 7: Submission Packaging

Objective: Prepare the blinded journal submission package.

Prepare:

- Blinded manuscript.
- Separate title page.
- Cover letter.
- Declarations.
- Supplementary files and appendices if allowed/needed.

Check:

- File names and document titles are consistent.
- Self-citations and acknowledgments are handled for double-anonymized review.
- Cover letter makes claims no stronger than the manuscript.

## Stage 8: Final QA

Objective: Run a package-level review before upload.

Check:

- Current official JBR formatting requirements.
- Abstract, introduction, discussion, and cover letter tell the same story.
- No unsupported strong claim remains.
- No blinding leak remains.
- All files required by the submission portal are present.

Decision rules:

- If Stage 1 reveals a fatal research question problem, do not move directly to polishing.
- If Stage 2 cannot produce a stable central claim, do not rewrite the abstract yet.
- If Stage 4 shows evidence weaker than rhetoric, soften the rhetoric before submission.
- If Stage 8 finds major contradictions, do not submit.
