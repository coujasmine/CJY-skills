# Abstract Revision Examples

Each example shows:

1. **BEFORE** — a typical weak abstract pattern
2. **AFTER** — a JBR-calibrated rewrite (≤150 words)
3. **ANNOTATION** — line-by-line reasoning for each substantive change
4. **WORD COUNT** check

Use these as *patterns to imitate*, not as claims to copy. The substantive content (firm names, theory, findings) is illustrative.

---

## Example 1 — AI capability and managerial myopia (archival panel design)

### BEFORE (148 words; reads as a literature gap, not a business problem)

> This study examines the impact of artificial intelligence (AI) on managerial myopia. Although prior studies have explored the role of AI in firms, few have investigated how AI affects executives' temporal orientation. Drawing on multiple theories including the attention-based view, dynamic capabilities, and resource-based view, we hypothesize that AI improves managerial decision-making. Using data from Chinese listed firms during 2014–2024, we find that AI causes a reduction in managerial myopia. This effect is moderated by short-horizon incentives, data infrastructure, and industry competition. Our results suggest that AI is the first technology to systematically reshape executive attention horizons. This paper enriches the literature on AI and corporate governance, and offers important implications for managers, policymakers, and other stakeholders interested in long-term firm performance.

### AFTER (149 words; business problem, mechanism, calibrated claim)

> Firms increasingly invest in artificial intelligence (AI) capabilities to support strategic decisions, yet returns to AI vary widely, and managers often remain focused on short-term outcomes. Why some AI investments shift managers' temporal attention while others do not remains unclear. Drawing on the attention-based view, we propose that *discovery-oriented* AI capability building reshapes the structural distribution of executive attention and is therefore associated with reduced managerial temporal myopia. Using panel data on 2,100 Chinese A-share listed firms from 2014 to 2024 and a sentence-level recruitment-text measure of discovery AI activity, we find that discovery-oriented AI is negatively associated with myopia, and that the association is weaker when short-horizon incentives are strong and stronger when data infrastructure is well developed. The study contributes to the attention-based view by specifying an attention-infrastructure mechanism and informs managers seeking to extend their planning horizons.

### ANNOTATION

| Line | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Replaced "This study examines…" with "Firms increasingly invest in AI capabilities…" | Hard Rule: open with a business problem, not a literature gap (see `jbr_house_style.md` §10; `jbr_section_rewrite_playbook.md` §1) |
| Tension | Added "yet returns to AI vary widely, and managers often remain focused on short-term outcomes" | Creates the puzzle without listing a gap; shows the practical tension JBR readers care about |
| Theory framing | Cut "multiple theories including … and resource-based view"; kept only attention-based view | One primary conversation, per `jbr_section_rewrite_playbook.md` §3; three-theory salads dilute contribution |
| Construct sharpening | Replaced generic "AI" with "discovery-oriented AI capability building" | Distinguishes the focal construct from broad "AI" — addresses construct contamination risk |
| Claim verb | Replaced "causes" with "is associated with" | Panel data with FE is association, not causation, per `jbr_claim_evidence_matrix.md` (cross-sectional/panel FE row); cuts desk-reject trigger E1 |
| Data specificity | Added "2,100 Chinese A-share listed firms … sentence-level recruitment-text measure" | Replaces vague "data from firms" with replication-sufficient detail |
| Moderators | Cut industry competition (only the two strongest moderators retained) | Abstract real estate is scarce; lead with the moderators that carry the contribution |
| Firstness claim | Removed "first technology to systematically reshape" | No supporting evidence for the claim; triggers Hard Rule 5 and desk-reject trigger F3 |
| Contribution | Replaced "enriches the literature" with "specifying an attention-infrastructure mechanism" | Names the specific theoretical movement (mechanism specification); satisfies `jbr_section_rewrite_playbook.md` §1 contribution requirement |
| Implications | Replaced "managers, policymakers, and other stakeholders" with "managers seeking to extend their planning horizons" | Specific audience tied to the mechanism; avoids generic stakeholder list |

**Word count check**: 149/150 ✓
**Causal verb check**: "is associated with," "reshapes" — both supported by panel FE design ✓
**Theory primacy check**: ABV is the only primary lens ✓

## Example 2 — AI adoption and employee wellbeing (cross-sectional survey)

### BEFORE (151 words; over-claims causality on cross-sectional data)

> Despite the rapid spread of artificial intelligence in workplaces, little is known about the effects of AI on employee wellbeing. Drawing on Job Demands-Resources theory, we examine how AI adoption causes changes in employee wellbeing and identify the mediating roles of task optimization and workplace safety. Using survey data from 412 employees across multiple industries, we find that AI directly improves employee wellbeing. Furthermore, this positive effect is fully mediated by task optimization and workplace safety. Our results indicate that organizations adopting AI can substantially improve their employees' wellbeing. This paper makes important contributions to the literature on technology and human resources management and offers significant implications for managers concerned about employee outcomes in the age of AI.

### AFTER (148 words; null direct effect honestly reported and used to sharpen contribution)

> Firms adopt artificial intelligence (AI) at scale, yet employee surveys consistently show ambivalent reactions — improved efficiency alongside concerns about surveillance, deskilling, and stress. Whether and how AI improves employee wellbeing therefore remains unresolved. Drawing on Job Demands-Resources theory, we propose that AI adoption does not directly affect employee wellbeing but operates indirectly by reshaping task design (task optimization) and workplace safety. Using cross-sectional survey data from 412 employees across knowledge-intensive industries, we find no direct association between AI adoption and wellbeing, but a positive indirect association through task optimization and workplace safety. The findings suggest that AI's benefits for employees are not automatic; they depend on whether AI implementation redesigns work in ways that change job resources and demands. The study contributes to research on workplace AI by relocating wellbeing effects from direct to mechanism-mediated, and offers implementation guidance for managers.

### ANNOTATION

| Line | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Replaced "Despite the rapid spread … little is known" with "Firms adopt AI at scale, yet employee surveys consistently show ambivalent reactions" | Opens with phenomenon + tension, not a counting gap |
| Theory framing | Kept JD-R as primary | Already correct in BEFORE — no change |
| Causal claim | Replaced "causes changes" with "operates indirectly by reshaping" | Cross-sectional survey cannot support "causes"; row in `jbr_claim_evidence_matrix.md` |
| Findings | Replaced "AI directly improves … fully mediated" with "no direct association, but a positive indirect association" | The BEFORE buries a null direct effect; the AFTER reports it honestly and uses it to sharpen the mechanism contribution (see `jbr_exemplar_patterns_2025_ai.md` Exemplar 1) |
| Contribution | Replaced "important contributions … significant implications" with "relocating wellbeing effects from direct to mechanism-mediated" | Specific theoretical movement (mechanism specification) |
| Implications | "implementation guidance for managers" instead of generic "managers concerned about employee outcomes" | Tied to the actual mechanism (work redesign), not a generic audience hand-wave |
| Removed | "substantially improve their employees' wellbeing" | Over-claim relative to a null direct effect; would trigger reviewer pushback |

**Word count check**: 148/150 ✓
**Causal verb check**: "operates indirectly," "is positively associated with" — both calibrated to survey design ✓
**Null finding handled honestly** ✓

## Example 3 — Multi-case qualitative study (theory-extending)

### BEFORE (152 words; claims statistical generalization from cases)

> Although servitization has become an important strategy for manufacturing firms, the mechanisms by which firms transform from product to service providers remain poorly understood. This paper investigates how manufacturing firms achieve successful servitization. Using a multi-case study of six European manufacturers, we find that firms that engage in three key activities — capability reconfiguration, customer co-creation, and digital platform integration — successfully transition to servitization. Firms that engage in these activities significantly outperform their competitors. Our findings prove that servitization requires these three activities and generalize broadly to manufacturing firms across industries. The study has important implications for theory and practice.

### AFTER (146 words; theory-extending claim with analytic generalization)

> Manufacturing firms increasingly attempt to shift from selling products to selling outcome-based services, yet the organizational transitions involved are uneven and frequently fail. Drawing on the dynamic capabilities view, we ask which configurations of activities support a manufacturer's move toward outcome-based service provision. Using a multi-case design with six European manufacturers (three high-success, three stalled transitions), we find that high-success cases combine capability reconfiguration, customer co-creation, and digital platform integration into mutually reinforcing routines, whereas stalled cases pursue these activities in isolation. The findings suggest that servitization success rests on the **interaction** among the three activities rather than their individual presence, extending dynamic capabilities theory toward configurational logic in service transitions. We do not claim statistical generalization; the study supports analytic generalization to theory. Implications for managers include sequencing the three activities for mutual reinforcement.

### ANNOTATION

| Line | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Replaced "Although servitization has become an important strategy … remain poorly understood" with "Manufacturing firms increasingly attempt to shift … yet the organizational transitions involved are uneven and frequently fail" | Business tension, not literature gap |
| Theory | Added "Drawing on the dynamic capabilities view" | Names a primary theory (BEFORE was atheoretical) |
| Case design | Replaced "six European manufacturers" with "six European manufacturers (three high-success, three stalled transitions)" | Adds theoretical sampling logic — addresses desk-reject trigger E5 (qual without case-selection logic) |
| Findings | Replaced "engage in three key activities … successfully transition" with "high-success cases combine [the three] into mutually reinforcing routines, whereas stalled cases pursue these activities in isolation" | Configurational claim (the actual contribution), not three-ingredient checklist |
| Claim verb | Replaced "prove … generalize broadly" with "suggest … extending dynamic capabilities theory" | Qual studies support **analytic** generalization, not statistical (see `jbr_claim_evidence_matrix.md` multi-case row) |
| Generalization | Added "We do not claim statistical generalization; the study supports analytic generalization to theory" | Defuses the most common reviewer comment on qual papers |
| Contribution | Named "configurational logic in service transitions" | Specific theoretical movement (boundary / configuration) |
| Implications | "sequencing the three activities for mutual reinforcement" | Specific managerial action tied to the mechanism |

**Word count check**: 146/150 ✓
**Causal verb check**: "suggest," "extending," "supports" — all calibrated to multi-case design ✓
**Analytic generalization explicit** ✓

## Common patterns across all three rewrites

1. **Replace literature-gap opening with business-tension opening** — every BEFORE opens with "Despite/Although/While few studies have"; every AFTER opens with a concrete firm/market problem.
2. **Cut multi-theory salads** — keep one primary theoretical lens.
3. **Sharpen the focal construct** — generic "AI" becomes "discovery-oriented AI capability building"; generic "servitization activities" becomes "configurations of three activities."
4. **Match claim verb to design** — every BEFORE overshoots; every AFTER matches the matrix.
5. **Handle nulls honestly** — when a direct effect is null, lead with the indirect mechanism.
6. **Name the contribution specifically** — "enriches the literature" becomes "specifying an attention-infrastructure mechanism" / "relocating effects from direct to mechanism-mediated" / "configurational logic in service transitions."
7. **Tie implications to the mechanism** — generic stakeholders become specific audiences with specific actions.

When you polish a user's abstract, run through this checklist and ensure every move is present.
