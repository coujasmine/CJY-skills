## A. Cover letter for first submission

### Length

1 page is typical. The cover letter is not the place to re-pitch the paper — the abstract does that. The cover letter confirms compliance and signals fit.

### Structure

1. **Salutation** — Address by name if known (e.g., "Dear Editor Csaszar" for the recent SI; otherwise "Dear Editor-in-Chief"). For SI: "Dear Guest Editors of the [SI Title]".

2. **Submission statement** — One sentence: "We are pleased to submit our manuscript titled '[Title]' for consideration as an article (or special-issue article) at Strategy Science."

3. **Fit and contribution** — 2-3 sentences: state the paper's research question, theoretical lineage, and primary contribution. Connect explicitly to SS's scope (theory-forward strategy, Carnegie/cognitive/formal/ecosystem traditions). For SI: explicitly map to SI themes.

4. **Originality and compliance statement** — 1-2 sentences:
   - The manuscript is not under review elsewhere
   - The manuscript has not been previously published
   - All authors have approved the submission
   - All required disclosures (AI use, COI, funding, IRB, pre-registration) are completed
   - The manuscript has been blinded per double-anonymous review

5. **Closing** — One sentence: "We look forward to your decision."

6. **Signature** — Corresponding author name + title + affiliation + email

### Template

```
[Date]

Dear Editor [Name] / Guest Editors of [SI Name]:

We are pleased to submit our manuscript titled "[Title]" for consideration
as an article at *Strategy Science* [for the special issue on "[SI Theme]"].

In this paper, we [one-sentence statement of RQ]. Drawing on [primary
theoretical lineage with anchor citation], we [briefly state design + key
finding]. Our paper extends the [lineage] literature by [specific theoretical
movement 1] and [specific theoretical movement 2]. We believe the manuscript
is a strong fit for Strategy Science's [behavioral / formal / ecosystem /
cognitive / etc.] research agenda [and the special issue's focus on X].

This manuscript is not under review elsewhere and has not been previously
published. All authors have approved the submission. The manuscript has been
prepared for double-anonymous review with author identifiers placed on a
separate title page. We have included AI-use, conflict-of-interest, funding,
and data availability disclosures per INFORMS policy. [Add IRB statement if
applicable: This study received IRB approval from [Institution; Project ID].]
[Add pre-registration statement if applicable: This study was pre-registered
at [anonymized OSF link]; see Methods.]

We look forward to your decision and welcome any questions.

Sincerely,

[Corresponding Author Name]
[Title], [Affiliation]
[Email]
```

### Principles

- **No flattery**: do not praise the journal or the editor's prior work.
- **No exaggeration**: do not claim "novel", "groundbreaking", "first of its kind" without backing.
- **Concrete fit**: name the SS lineage, not just "strategy".
- **Compliance is explicit**: don't say "we have done everything" — list the specific items.
- **No comparison to other outlets**: don't say "we considered SMJ but think SS is better."

## B. Cover letter for revision (R&R)

### Length

1 page, optionally 1.5 with a "summary of changes" paragraph.

### Structure

1. **Salutation** — Same editor (or new AE if assigned).

2. **Resubmission statement** — "We are pleased to resubmit our revised manuscript titled '[Title]' (manuscript ID: [ID]) for consideration as an article at Strategy Science."

3. **Round and timeline** — "We have responded to all comments from the Editor, the Associate Editor, and Reviewers 1, 2, and 3."

4. **Summary of major changes** — 3-5 bullets summarizing the most significant revisions. Do NOT enumerate every change — that's what the response letter is for.

5. **Pointer to response letter** — "A detailed point-by-point response is attached as a separate document."

6. **Compliance statement (brief)** — One sentence affirming continued compliance.

7. **Closing**

### Template

```
[Date]

Dear Editor [Name]:

We are pleased to resubmit our revised manuscript titled "[Title]" (manuscript
ID: [STSC-YYYY-NNNN]) for consideration as an article at *Strategy Science*.
We have responded to all comments from the Editor, the Associate Editor, and
Reviewers 1, 2, and 3.

Major revisions in this version include:

- [Revision 1, e.g., "Re-anchored the theoretical contribution against
  Csaszar & Laureiro-Martínez (2018) per Reviewer 1's request"]
- [Revision 2, e.g., "Added a within-firm fixed-effects specification and
  manager-level fixed-effects robustness check per Reviewer 2's request"]
- [Revision 3, e.g., "Restructured the discussion to engage Felin & Holweg
  (2024) and Csaszar et al. (2024b) on AI in strategic decision-making"]
- [Revision 4, e.g., "Added an Appendix with full LLM prompts and
  validation procedure"]

A detailed point-by-point response to all reviewer and editor comments is
provided as a separate document.

We continue to confirm that the manuscript is original, not under review
elsewhere, and meets all INFORMS disclosure requirements.

We thank the Editor, Associate Editor, and Reviewers for the thoughtful and
constructive engagement. We look forward to your decision.

Sincerely,

[Corresponding Author Name]
[Title], [Affiliation]
[Email]
```

## C. Point-by-point response to reviewers

### Length

5-30 pages depending on the number and depth of comments. There is no hard cap, but be efficient — long responses signal defensive engagement.

### Structure

1. **Opening greeting and orientation** — 1 paragraph thanking the editor and reviewers; orient the reader to the response structure.

2. **Editor / AE response** — Address editor comments first.

3. **Reviewer 1 response** — Quote each comment verbatim, then respond, then state the manuscript change with page/line/section reference.

4. **Reviewer 2 response** — Same pattern.

5. **Reviewer 3 response** — Same pattern.

### Per-comment template

```
**Comment R1.1.** [Verbatim quotation of the reviewer's comment, in italic
or block-quote format]

**Response:** [Substantive response that:
- (a) Acknowledges the reviewer's point
- (b) Explains what was done (or why not, with rationale)
- (c) References the specific manuscript change(s) with page/line/section
- (d) If disagreeing, presents evidence, not defensiveness]

**Manuscript change:** [What changed and where: e.g., "We added a paragraph
to Section 2.3 (pages 8-9) addressing the cognitive-overload counter-
argument, citing Dane (2010) and Haas & Ham (2015)."]
```

### Principles

1. **Quote verbatim.** Always paste the reviewer's exact words. Do not paraphrase.
2. **One comment = one response.** Do not lump multiple comments together.
3. **Take every comment seriously.** Even short ones (e.g., "fix typo on p. 14") deserve a "Done" or "Corrected on page 14" response.
4. **Reference the manuscript explicitly.** "We added a paragraph" is not enough — say "page 12, paragraph 2" or "Section 3.4."
5. **Disagree carefully.** If you disagree, present evidence (theoretical or empirical), not defensiveness. Acknowledge the reviewer's perspective first. Example:
   - ✓ "Reviewer 2 raises an important concern about reverse causality. We agree this is a serious issue and have addressed it in three ways. First, [evidence]. Second, [evidence]. Third, [evidence]. Together, these analyses suggest that reverse causality is unlikely to drive our results, though we acknowledge it cannot be fully ruled out in our observational design and have added this to the limitations section (page 27)."
   - ✗ "Reviewer 2 misunderstands our identification strategy. As we already stated on page 18..."
6. **Don't be defensive.** Reviewers are usually trying to help. Even when their suggestions seem off-base, respond constructively.
7. **Don't add new content without disclosure.** If you change the theory or add a new analysis, flag it clearly so reviewers can find it.
8. **AI-use disclosure for responses.** If AI was used to draft the response, disclose this in the cover letter.

### Opening greeting template

```
We are grateful to the Editor, the Associate Editor, and Reviewers 1, 2, and
3 for the thoughtful and constructive feedback. The comments have substantially
improved the manuscript, particularly in [areas, e.g., "the sharpness of our
theoretical contribution and the rigor of our identification strategy"].

In this response, we address each comment in turn. Reviewer comments appear
in italic font; our responses follow each comment. References to revised
sections, pages, and lines refer to the revised manuscript with track changes.

We have done our best to address every point. Where we have chosen a different
approach than the one suggested by a reviewer, we explain our reasoning and
welcome further discussion.
```

## D. Common SS reviewer-response patterns

### Pattern 1: Theoretical contribution sharpening

When R1 says "the contribution is not sharp enough":

```
We thank Reviewer 1 for pressing us to articulate the contribution more
sharply. In the revision, we have:

(a) Re-anchored the theoretical contribution against [closest prior work],
naming two specific movements rather than three general claims (page 4,
paragraphs 5-6).

(b) Engaged [specific lineage] more directly in the discussion, explicitly
distinguishing our contribution from [prior paper X] (Section 5.2, pages 24-25).

(c) Added a paragraph in the introduction that names what our framework can
explain that prior work cannot (page 5, paragraph 4).

We hope these revisions clarify the contribution and demonstrate the
theoretical movement we are making.
```

### Pattern 2: Identification strategy strengthening

When R2 says "the causal language overruns the design":

```
We agree with Reviewer 2 that our original phrasing implied stronger causal
claims than our observational design supports. We have made two changes:

(a) Throughout the manuscript, we have softened causal language. We now use
"is associated with" / "predicts" / "is followed by" rather than "causes" /
"leads to" / "drives" (revised throughout — see track changes for full sweep).

(b) We have added a paragraph to the Discussion (Section 5.4, pages 26-27)
that explicitly discusses the identification limitations of our design and
the conditions under which our findings would generalize causally.

We have also added two additional robustness checks: [check 1] and [check 2]
(Appendix B), which address [specific threats Reviewer 2 raised].
```

### Pattern 3: Measurement validation

When R2 asks about LLM-as-measurement validity:

```
We appreciate Reviewer 2's careful attention to measurement validity. We
have strengthened our measurement procedure in three ways:

(a) Added a human-coder benchmark on a subset of N = [size] items, reporting
Krippendorff's α = [value] between LLM and human coding (Section 3.X, page Y).

(b) Implemented a sensitivity analysis using two additional LLMs ([model 2]
and [model 3]) with inter-rater agreement r = [value] (Section 3.X, page Y).

(c) Documented the full LLM prompts and procedure in a new Appendix [X],
including the version pins, batch sizes, and disagreement-resolution procedure.

These additions follow the validation procedure used in [Kanis et al. 2026]
and others in this literature.
```

### Pattern 4: Disagreement with reviewer

When the reviewer's suggestion conflicts with the paper's design:

```
Reviewer 3 suggests that we expand our experiment to include additional
conditions (specifically, [reviewer's suggestion]). We thank the reviewer
for this thoughtful idea and discussed it carefully.

However, after consideration, we have chosen not to add this condition for
the following reasons:

(a) [Theoretical reason: the proposed condition is not central to our
research question.]

(b) [Practical reason: the proposed condition would require [specific
trade-off, e.g., 100+ additional participants, which is beyond the scope of
this paper].]

(c) [Future research framing: we have added the proposed condition to the
Future Research section (Section 5.4, page 27) as a direction we explicitly
recommend for follow-up work.]

We hope this approach addresses the spirit of the reviewer's concern while
maintaining the focused scope of the current paper.
```

## E. Response letter common mistakes

1. **Quoting the reviewer in full but responding briefly.** Each comment deserves a substantive response.
2. **Lumping multiple comments together.** "We address R2.1 and R2.2 together..." — usually a sign that one of them wasn't fully addressed.
3. **Defensive language.** "As we already explained...", "The reviewer misunderstands...", "This is a standard practice..." — all problematic.
4. **Vague manuscript pointers.** "We have added a paragraph to the introduction" without a page number.
5. **Promises without delivery.** "We agree this should be done in future work" — make sure the future research section actually addresses it.
6. **New analyses without explanation.** Don't surprise the reviewer with new content; explain why it was added.
7. **Inconsistent tone.** Some reviewers get warm responses, others get curt. Maintain a consistent professional tone throughout.

## F. Tips for difficult R&R situations

### Multiple-round R&R

If you're at round 2 or 3, the reviewers' patience is limited. Address every concern fully and avoid raising new issues. The cover letter should explicitly note "this is round [N] of revisions" and the manuscript ID.

### Conflicting reviewer recommendations

If R1 says "make it shorter" and R2 says "add more analysis":

- Address both: trim some content AND add the requested analysis (perhaps in an appendix)
- Acknowledge the tension in the cover letter
- Let the AE adjudicate

### Reviewer asks for something impossible

If a reviewer asks for data or analysis that genuinely cannot be done:

- Explain clearly why it cannot be done (data unavailable, ethically prohibited, beyond scope)
- Offer the next-best alternative
- Frame as a future-research opportunity

### Editor's letter conflicts with reviewers

If the AE's letter contradicts a reviewer's suggestion:

- Follow the AE's guidance (they have the recommendation power)
- Note the conflict explicitly in the response: "Reviewer 1 suggested X. The Associate Editor's letter indicated Y. We have followed Y..."

## G. AI-use disclosure for cover letters and response letters

If AI was used to draft any part of the cover letter or response letter:

- Disclose in the cover letter (not in the response letter, which goes to reviewers)
- Example: "We used [LLM] in drafting portions of this response letter. All AI-generated text has been reviewed by all authors, and we take full responsibility for the content."
