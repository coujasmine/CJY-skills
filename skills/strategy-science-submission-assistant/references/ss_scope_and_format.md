## Journal Identity

*Strategy Science* (SS) is an INFORMS journal launched in 2016. ISSN: 2333-2050 (print), 2333-2077 (online). The current issue page lists Daniel A. Levinthal as Editor-in-Chief. The March 2026 special issue "Can AI Do Strategy?" was edited by Felipe A. Csaszar, Gwendolyn Lee, Peter Zemsky, and Todd Zenger.

SS publishes research on strategic management using a broad range of approaches. The official editorial statement welcomes work that develops new theories or substantially extends existing ones, develops empirical facts that challenge established theory or require uncommon empirical approaches, or brings together fields that can learn from each other. Suitable methods include field-based empirical work, large-sample empirical studies, computational or analytic models, experiments, and theory development.

Common SS lineages include behavioral strategy, Carnegie School, cognitive strategy, formal modeling, ecosystems, governance, dynamic capabilities, and strategic human capital. These are calibration anchors, not the journal's full boundary.

SS does NOT publish:
- Generic OB or HR studies without a strategic outcome
- Pure marketing or finance work without strategy mechanisms
- Methodology-only papers
- Replications without a theoretical extension
- Descriptive empirical work without theoretical movement

## Scope Fit

SS fit requires more than a strategy topic. The manuscript should:

- Engage a **strategic decision, mechanism, or architecture** (e.g., foresight, knowledge breadth, ecosystem governance, acquisition decision-making, mental representations, coordination).
- Position against a **named theoretical lineage** that SS routinely publishes in. Major lineages include:
  - **Carnegie School / behavioral theory of the firm**: Cyert & March 1963; Simon 1947, 1957, 1997; Gavetti, Greve, Levinthal & Ocasio 2012
  - **Mental representations / cognitive strategy / strategic foresight**: Csaszar 2018; Csaszar & Laureiro-Martínez 2018; Gavetti & Menon 2016; Gavetti & Levinthal 2000; Kapoor & Wilde 2023; Peterson & Wu 2021
  - **Strategic human capital**: Castanias & Helfat 2001; Helfat & Peteraf 2015; Custódio et al. 2013, 2019; Mannucci & Yong 2018
  - **Innovation ecosystems / platforms / architecture**: Adner & Kapoor 2010; Jacobides, Cennamo & Gawer 2018; Baldwin 2024; Hannah & Eisenhardt 2018
  - **Dynamic capabilities**: Teece, Pisano & Shuen 1997; Helfat & Peteraf 2015
  - **Transaction cost economics / hybrid governance**: Williamson 1985, 1991; Argyres
  - **Formal/game-theoretic coordination**: Schelling 1980; Farrell & Saloner 1988; Simcoe & Watson 2019
  - **AI in strategic decision-making (emerging)**: use verified AI-strategy anchors from the user's bibliography or current SS special-issue articles; do not insert exact author-year citations from memory
- Offer a **theoretical movement** — not merely "we find X in setting Y." Acceptable movements (Whetten 1989 vocabulary):
  - Mechanism specification ("we explicate the cognitive mechanism by which...")
  - Boundary refinement ("we identify conditions under which...")
  - Integration ("we integrate X and Y to show...")
  - Reconciliation ("we reconcile competing findings of X and Y by...")
  - Extension ("we extend X to a new domain Z, with implications for...")
  - New construct ("we introduce the construct of X, distinct from Y, to capture...")
- Provide **rigorous evidence calibrated to the claim**: archival panel + identification, experimental design with manipulation checks, formal model with stated assumptions, etc.

Weak fit signals:

- The paper is a generic management or social-science paper that happens to be about a firm.
- The contribution is only "this relationship has not been studied" or "we extend X to industry Y" without a mechanism.
- The empirical work is interesting but the theory is bolted on (a JBR pattern that does not survive SS).
- The paper has formal modeling but no strategic-management implication.
- Implications are generic ("managers should pay attention").

## Current Formatting Norms

Use these as submission QA checks, then re-check the official author guide and INFORMS portal:

### Basic format

- **Official length expectation: no more than 35 pages all-inclusive.** If longer, the cover letter should briefly justify the exception; the journal can host online supplements.
- **Official manuscript format:** double-spaced, 11-point standard font such as Times New Roman, with 1-inch margins on all sides.
- Authors may use any software as long as they can upload a PDF in ScholarOne; the system also allows `.doc` and `.docx` files.

### Abstract

- **Official limit for the manuscript abstract: no more than 200 words.**
- **Official limit for the ScholarOne metadata abstract field: no more than 250 words.**
- Published article abstracts may be longer than the current submission guide permits; use them for rhetorical patterning, not for compliance thresholds.
- Structure: research question / theoretical motivation (1-2 sentences) -> theoretical lens or mechanism (1-2 sentences) -> empirical design or analytical approach (1-2 sentences) -> key findings / propositions (2-3 sentences) -> contribution statement (1 sentence).
- Do not include section headings or citations inside the abstract.

### Keywords

- **Official range: 3-10 keywords.** Comma-, semicolon-, or portal-field separated.
- Exemplar keyword sets:
  - Kanis et al.: artificial intelligence; large language models; mental representations; strategic foresight; strategic decision-making; time constraints (6)
  - Qu et al.: artificial intelligence; strategic decision making; acquisitions; strategic foresight; machine learning; market reactions (6)
  - Clough: architecture; coordination; decentralization; ecosystem; governance; network; organization design; platform (8 — high end)
  - Asghar: human capital; knowledge breadth; strategic foresight; insider trading; new product introductions (5)

### Manuscript structure (standard empirical paper)

1. **Introduction** — opens with the strategic problem or theoretical puzzle, names the parent theory, articulates the RQ, previews the contribution. Roughly 4-6 pages. No section number for "Introduction" in the original Word file, but appears as Section 1 once formatted.
2. **Theory and Hypotheses** (or *Theoretical Background* for pure-theory papers) — Section 2, with named subsections (2.1, 2.2, ...). Each hypothesis is **bolded and labeled** (e.g., **Hypothesis 1a (H1a).** *Italicized statement.*).
3. **Methods** (or *Sample and Methods*) — Section 3. Standard subsections: 3.1 Data and Sample, 3.2 Dependent/Independent Variables, 3.3 Control Variables, 3.4 Econometric Model (or for experiments: Task, Design and Manipulation, Procedure, Measures, Participants).
4. **Results** — Section 4. Standard order: descriptive statistics → correlations → main test → robustness → additional analyses.
5. **Discussion** (or *Discussion and Conclusion*) — Section 5. Standard subsections: 5.1 Overview, 5.2 Theoretical/Practical Implications, 5.3 Limitations and Future Research (or 5.3 Future Directions).
6. **References** — INFORMS style (see below), alphabetized by author.
7. **Tables and figures** — numbered consecutively, cited in the text, and grouped together after the references for submission.
8. **Appendices / online supplements** — Appendix A, B, C... when extensive; use online supplements/e-companions for material that would push the paper past the 35-page expectation.
9. **Acknowledgments and other identifying material** — remove from the blinded manuscript and restore after acceptance or place only in author-identifying metadata/title-page material if requested by ScholarOne.

Official note policy: endnotes and footnotes should be avoided. If a note is necessary, keep it brief and indicate it within the text rather than building a separate notes apparatus.

For **pure-theory papers** (Clough 2026 style), the structure may be:
1. Introduction → 2. Coordination Challenges (or theoretical setup) → 3. Governance Structures (core building blocks) → 4. From Building Blocks to Ideal Types → 5. Discriminating Alignment Framework → 6. Discussion → Conclusion.

### Hypothesis labeling

```
**Hypothesis 1a (H1a).** *Managers with greater industry knowledge breadth are
associated with less foresight regarding the performance of product introductions
than those who have narrower industry knowledge breadth.*
```

- Bold "Hypothesis 1a (H1a)."
- Italicize the statement.
- Indent (typically 0.5 inch or block-quote style).
- Use sub-letters (1a, 1b, 1c) for sub-hypotheses under the same parent prediction.

### Citations and references (INFORMS style)

- **In-text:** parenthetical author-year, NO comma between author and year (e.g., "(Csaszar 2018)" not "(Csaszar, 2018)"; "(Gavetti and Levinthal 2000)" not "(Gavetti & Levinthal, 2000)").
- For 3+ authors: "(Author et al. year)" (e.g., "(Csaszar et al. 2024b)").
- Multiple authors in same parenthesis separated by comma, ordered by year then alphabet: "(Csaszar 2018, Gavetti and Menon 2016)".
- **Reference list:** alphabetical by first author, INFORMS style:
  ```
  Csaszar FA, Laureiro-Martínez D (2018) Individual and organizational antecedents of strategic foresight: A representational approach. Strategy Sci. 3(3):513–532.
  ```
- Journal titles are abbreviated INFORMS-style (Strategy Sci., Acad. Management J., Strategic Management J., Organ. Sci., Manage. Sci., J. Econom., Admin. Sci. Quart.).
- DOI optional, page range required.

### Tables and figures

- Official submission rule: tables and figures should be numbered consecutively and grouped together after the references.
- Cite the intended position of each table or figure in the text.
- "Table 1." / "Figure 1." centered headers.
- Notes below each table starting with "*Notes.*" (italic).
- Significance markers: \*\*\* p < 0.001, \*\* p < 0.01, \* p < 0.05, † p < 0.10 (with stated p-value direction — one-sided or two-sided).
- Standard errors in parentheses below coefficients (SS norm; not t-statistics).

### Text and figure source files

Use these INFORMS file-preparation checks when packaging accepted files or doing a production-risk audit:

- All manuscript and figure source files must use embedded standard fonts; do not use custom fonts.
- Text source files should be Microsoft Word or LaTeX/TeX. Word files should be accompanied by a PDF. LaTeX is encouraged for extensive math content and may be required by some editors.
- Original images must be at least 300 dpi and will be converted to CMYK or grayscale.
- Original graphs and drawings should be editable vector graphics when possible.
- Acceptable figure source formats:
  - EPS for charts, graphs, and drawings; maximum 1 MB.
  - TIFF for photographs and screenshots.
  - High-quality PDF for images/graphs, with embedded fonts and image quality maintained at 600 dpi or higher; the file should be editable, not a bitmap.
  - MS Word or PowerPoint for charts/graphs when the figures are included in the accepted PDF for comparison.
  - Adobe Illustrator with layers flattened.
  - Microsoft Excel plus an editable PDF of the graph/figure.
- For color figures, check grayscale conversion before submission. By default, figures convert to grayscale for print unless authors pay approved print-color charges.
- Do not rely only on color to distinguish lines or points; use line types, weights, point shapes, and light/dark contrast.
- Avoid background shading and 3-D graphics.
- For screenshots, enlarge the display before capture and prefer lossless TIFF over JPG when possible.

### Blinding

- Peer review is **double-anonymous** at SS.
- ScholarOne collects author and institution information during submission. Use a separate title page or author-identifying file only when the portal or editor requests it; the blinded manuscript itself must be scrubbed of:
  - Author names
  - Affiliations
  - Acknowledgments (move to title page or post-acceptance)
  - Self-citations phrased as "in our prior work" (rewrite as third-person)
  - Funding clues that name the authors' institutions
  - File metadata (Word/PDF document properties)
  - IRB statements that name the institution
- Pre-registration links should be anonymized (use OSF anonymous view link) until acceptance.

### Article-history block

Each published SS article has a "History" block on the first page:

```
Received: April 29, 2025
Revised: September 19, 2025
Accepted: December 14, 2025
Published Online in Articles in Advance: February 9, 2026
History: Accepted for the Special Issue: Can AI Do Strategy?
```

Authors do not write this themselves; it is added by INFORMS production. But it indicates that SS regularly runs special issues with explicit thematic gates — check whether your submission targets one.

### Open Access

- INFORMS Open Option is available for accepted articles; the official Strategy Science guide currently lists a **US$3,000** open-access fee.
- Standard subscription publication is default.
- If choosing OA, use the IOO publication license rather than the regular copyright transfer form and re-check the current fee before final files.

## Final QA Questions

- Is the manuscript abstract <=200 words, and is the ScholarOne abstract field <=250 words (use `scripts/check_abstract_word_count.py`)?
- Are keywords within the official 3-10 entry range (use `scripts/check_keywords_count.py`)?
- Is the manuscript double-spaced, 11-point standard font, with 1-inch margins?
- Are standard fonts embedded, with no custom fonts?
- Are hypotheses labeled in INFORMS bold-italic style?
- Are citations parenthetical author-year WITHOUT a comma?
- Are SE in parentheses (not t-statistics)?
- Is the manuscript blinded throughout, including acknowledgments, self-citations, and file metadata?
- Are author names/institutions supplied through ScholarOne and, if requested, in a separate title-page file rather than in the blinded manuscript?
- Do abstract, introduction, cover letter, and discussion describe the same theoretical contribution?
- Are all tables and figures cited in order and grouped after the references?
- Do figure source files meet INFORMS production specs: editable/vector when appropriate, images >=300 dpi, PDFs >=600 dpi with embedded fonts, and accepted formats (EPS/TIFF/PDF/Word/PowerPoint/Illustrator/Excel as applicable)?
- If figures use color, have they been checked in grayscale and are line/point distinctions still readable?
- Are references complete, INFORMS-formatted, and consistent with in-text citations?
- Are supplementary files named and referenced consistently?
- If AI was used, is a precise AI-use transparency statement prepared for the portal/cover letter/methods if requested or methodologically necessary?
- Is the IRB approval cited (for human subjects)?
- Is the pre-registration link anonymized (if pre-registered)?

## Special Issue Fit

SS runs special issues regularly. Recent examples:
- "Can AI Do Strategy?" (Vol. 11 No. 1, March 2026), guest edited by Felipe A. Csaszar, Gwendolyn Lee, Peter Zemsky, and Todd Zenger. The issue includes the editors' introduction, a dialogue/debate, and AI-strategy articles including Qu et al. 2026 and Kanis et al. 2026.

When the user targets a special issue:
- Ask for the call for papers or key text.
- Map the manuscript's research question, theory, method, and implications to the call's explicit themes. A vague "we touch on AI" is not fit; an explicit engagement with the SI's central problem is.
- Check the submission deadline against the call text. Do not infer expected review speed from a small set of published article histories.
- Do not claim fit based only on shared keywords.
- If fit is partial, recommend reframing the introduction and contribution around the call's central problem, OR retargeting to a regular SS issue with a less SI-specific framing.

## Comparison to adjacent outlets

If the manuscript is mis-fit for SS, possible alternates:
- **Strategic Management Journal (SMJ)** — more empirical, broader strategy scope, less Carnegie/cognitive-specific.
- **Organization Science** — strong overlap on cognition/learning, broader on org theory.
- **Academy of Management Journal (AMJ)** — empirical, more macro-OB / micro-OB overlap.
- **Academy of Management Discoveries (AMD)** — phenomenon-driven empirical; lower theory bar.
- **Journal of Business Research (JBR)** — broader business scope; lower theoretical-movement bar.
- **Journal of Management (JoM)** — empirical strategy and OB.

SS is **NOT** a good substitute for:
- **AMR** — pure conceptual papers are accepted at SS (Clough 2026), but AMR is the prototype outlet for pure theory in management.
- **Management Science** — formal modeling at MS is allowed, but MS is broader (OR/OM/marketing) and less Carnegie-flavored.
