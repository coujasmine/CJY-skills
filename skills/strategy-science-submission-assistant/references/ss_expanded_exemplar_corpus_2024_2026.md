# Strategy Science Expanded Exemplar Corpus (2024-2026)

## Use Rule

Load this file when a Strategy Science manuscript needs exemplar-guided
polishing, especially for AI and strategy, theory-based view, organizational
aggregation, M&A/resource reconfiguration, formal/theory-forward argumentation,
or introduction/contribution rewrites.

This file is a calibration layer. It teaches how recent SS papers build an
argument, sequence paragraphs, calibrate verbs, and frame theoretical movement.
Do not copy source wording into a user's manuscript. Use short terms only when
they are named constructs or titles, and verify source locators before using any
paper as claim support in a citation audit.

## 1. Csaszar and Eklund (2026): Assumption Repair Through Formal Aggregation

### When To Use This Exemplar

Use this profile when polishing work that:

- Challenges a canonical simplifying assumption in strategy.
- Builds a formal or analytical bridge from micro-level preferences to macro-level firm behavior.
- Needs to justify a new operational construct rather than simply name a conceptual gap.
- Claims to reconcile behavioral/microfoundational insights with tractable strategy models.

### Argument Architecture

1. Start from a core field assumption: many strategy models treat firms as
   coherent unitary actors.
2. Introduce the theoretical challenge: behavioral theory and microfoundations
   imply organizations are coalitions with heterogeneous and conflicting
   preferences.
3. Refine the gap: prior aggregation work predicts collective choices through
   choice probabilities, but strategy models typically need utility functions.
4. Pose a "how does X emerge from Y" research question.
5. Introduce primitives and a translation sequence: individual utilities,
   choice probabilities, aggregation structure, recovered organizational
   utility.
6. Derive simple cases first, then generalize to more complex structures.
7. Translate formal results back into strategy implications: risk preferences,
   competitive behavior, agency relationships, TMT links, and human-AI teams.

### Contribution Logic

The paper does not merely "add aggregation." It changes what a familiar
construct can mean. The contribution stack is:

- Establish organizational utility functions as meaningful but not simple
  averages of individual utilities.
- Provide a method for retrofitting unitary-actor theories with more realistic
  organizational preferences.
- Reconcile coalition-based views of the firm with analytically tractable
  strategy models.
- Open testable links between individual heterogeneity, aggregation structures,
  and macro organizational outcomes.

### Wording Calibration

Useful moves to emulate:

- "long-standing assumption" -> "behaviorally grounded foundation" -> "tractable analytical framework"
- "derive", "recover", "rationalize", "aggregate", "retrofit", "reconcile"
- "meaningful construct" when defending a construct's status.
- "not simple averages" when distinguishing aggregation from averaging.
- "as if" language for carefully bounded analytical simplification.

Avoid:

- Saying the paper proves firms are unitary actors. Its move is subtler: firms
  are not unitary internally, but a carefully derived organizational utility
  can represent collective behavior for some analyses.
- Treating aggregation structure as background. In this logic, structure is the
  mechanism.

## 2. Csaszar, Ketkar, and Kim (2024): AI Strategy as Tool, Evidence, and Framework

### When To Use This Exemplar

Use this profile when polishing work that:

- Studies AI or LLMs in strategic decision-making.
- Combines conceptual vignettes with empirical evidence.
- Needs balanced language about AI's current capabilities and future strategic
  implications.
- Connects AI to search, representation, aggregation, bounded rationality, or
  theory-based view.

### Argument Architecture

1. Define SDM as high-stakes, ambiguous, delayed-feedback, text-heavy work.
2. Explain why prior AI analogies are incomplete: rule-based prediction is not
   the same as open-ended strategic judgment.
3. Introduce LLMs as a change in feasibility, not as an automatic replacement
   for managers.
4. State multiple questions because the paper has multiple tasks: what
   AI-augmented SDM may look like, how effective current LLMs are, and what
   the implications are.
5. Use a three-part design: reimagine existing SDM tools, test LLMs against
   entrepreneurs/investors, then theorize implications for cognitive processes.
6. Build a framework that links AI use to SDM processes and competitive
   outcomes, rather than stopping at performance comparison.

### Contribution Logic

The paper earns SS fit by connecting evidence to a broader theory map:

- Vignettes make the phenomenon concrete.
- Empirical studies show LLMs can generate and evaluate strategies at a
  comparable level in realistic entrepreneurial settings.
- The framework ties AI to search, representation, and aggregation.
- The discussion keeps performance claims contingent on competitive dynamics
  and AI capability evolution.

### Wording Calibration

Useful moves to emulate:

- "may", "could", "potential", "as capabilities progress" for future-facing AI claims.
- "comparable with" rather than "better than" unless the evidence supports superiority.
- "realistic strategy environments" when defending task validity.
- "reimagined tools", "vignettes", "framework connecting", "emerging research frontier".
- "current LLMs" to avoid overgeneralizing from one model generation to all AI.

Avoid:

- Anthropomorphizing LLMs as understanding, intending, or knowing.
- Treating generated text quality as equivalent to firm performance.
- Claiming AI erases competitive advantage; the paper frames this as a scenario
  conditional on capability diffusion and strategic complementarity.

## 3. Felin and Holweg (2024): Contrarian Theory and Causal Reasoning

### When To Use This Exemplar

Use this profile when polishing work that:

- Makes a contrarian conceptual argument.
- Challenges AI-as-prediction or cognition-as-computation assumptions.
- Needs to defend theory-based view, causal reasoning, heterogeneous beliefs,
  experimentation, or novelty under uncertainty.
- Has no empirical test but must still feel rigorous, cumulative, and SS-fit.

### Argument Architecture

1. Begin with the dominant claim in the field and show why it matters.
2. State disagreement early and clearly.
3. Grant AI's strengths before narrowing the disagreement. This protects the
   argument from straw-manning.
4. Identify the hidden premise behind the dominant view: cognition as
   input-output computation and prediction from past data.
5. Replace the premise with a new mechanism: theory-based causal reasoning,
   heterogeneous beliefs, and directed experimentation.
6. Use an extended example to show the mechanism in action.
7. Return in the discussion to the limits of prediction in decision-making
   under uncertainty.

### Contribution Logic

The contribution is not "AI is bad." It is a conceptual boundary argument:

- Data-based prediction works well in routine or repetitive settings.
- Strategic decisions under uncertainty require forward-looking causal logic.
- Novelty arises when beliefs generate new data through intervention and
  experimentation.
- AI can still be useful as a tool, but its value depends on human theories,
  firm-specific data, and task matching.

### Wording Calibration

Useful moves to emulate:

- "we disagree", followed by a precise object of disagreement.
- "we grant that..." before narrowing the claim.
- "the problem is that..." to introduce a causal limitation.
- "not X, but Y" constructions for conceptual replacement.
- "forward-looking", "backward-looking", "theory-based causal logic",
  "heterogeneous beliefs", "directed experimentation", "data-belief asymmetry".

Avoid:

- A sweeping "AI cannot..." thesis. The paper repeatedly bounds the argument:
  AI is powerful for prediction and routine tasks, but limited for novelty and
  uncertainty.
- Turning the essay into a literature review. Its force comes from a replaced
  premise and a disciplined causal contrast.

## 4. Gibbs, Byun, and Lim (2026): Integrated Strategy Process Plus Identification

### When To Use This Exemplar

Use this profile when polishing work that:

- Studies M&A, divestiture, resource reconfiguration, or corporate strategy.
- Needs to integrate two literatures that treat decisions as separate.
- Uses an archival design where identification must be part of the theory
  story, not an afterthought.
- Develops moderators as mechanism tests.

### Argument Architecture

1. Start from a recognized strategic process: firms build, borrow, or buy
   resources and also redeploy or divest resources.
2. Identify the conceptual misspecification: growth and exit are often treated
   as sequential or separate, even though reconfiguration is iterative.
3. Introduce a disruptive event: acquisition termination.
4. Define the mechanism: a failed attempt to acquire a keystone resource can
   make related existing assets less valuable.
5. State the baseline hypothesis: failed acquisition increases target-related
   divestiture.
6. Develop moderators as mechanism probes: complementarity, target rejection,
   opportunity cost, and distance from core business.
7. Pair the theory with an identification strategy: compare terminated deals
   with completed or nondeal controls, including deal arbitrage risk matching.

### Contribution Logic

The paper's contribution is strong because each contribution moves a theory
boundary:

- Integrates growth and exit as simultaneous reconfiguration choices.
- Extends BBB by specifying a resource-gap type where existing assets depend on
  missing external complements.
- Adds resource complementarity as an antecedent to divestiture.
- Uses method innovation to make the empirical comparison more credible.

### Wording Calibration

Useful moves to emulate:

- "integrated, dynamic process" for linking previously separate choices.
- "parallel rather than sequential" when reframing decision architecture.
- "strategic inflection point" for a disruptive event that changes the choice set.
- "resource-level trigger", "keystone resource", "complementary assets",
  "opportunity cost", "noncore business".
- "if this effect arises from the proposed mechanism, it should be amplified
  when..." as the cleanest moderator-transition logic.

Avoid:

- Treating moderators as generic contingencies. In this paper, moderators are
  tests of whether the proposed mechanism is really operating.
- Reporting matching only as technical detail. The identification logic supports
  the theoretical claim that failure, not generic M&A propensity, drives the
  response.

## Cross-Paper Argument Lessons

### 1. Theoretical Tension Is a Mis-Specified Logic, Not a Missing Topic

Across these papers, the gap is not "few studies have examined X." The gap is:

- A canonical assumption is too simple for the phenomenon.
- A familiar decision sequence misses simultaneity.
- A dominant causal chain overweights prediction and underweights theory.
- A new technology creates uncertainty in established cognitive processes.

When polishing an SS introduction, convert topic gaps into logic gaps.

### 2. The Research Question Usually Follows a Translation Problem

Strong SS RQs ask how one level or process translates into another:

- Individual preferences -> organizational utility.
- AI use -> search, representation, aggregation -> strategic outcomes.
- Data and beliefs -> theory, experimentation, novelty.
- Acquisition failure -> resource portfolio exit.

When a user's RQ is broad, rewrite it around the translation mechanism.

### 3. Contribution Paragraphs Need Verbs That Do Theory Work

Prefer verbs that specify movement:

- "develop" a framework or method.
- "derive" a formal relationship.
- "reconcile" two views.
- "retrofit" a classic model.
- "integrate" separate literatures.
- "identify" a mechanism, boundary, or antecedent.
- "extend" a theory by specifying where or how it changes.

Avoid "contribute to", "add to", or "shed light on" unless followed by the
exact theoretical movement.

### 4. Methods Are Part of the Argument

In these exemplars, methods are not neutral description:

- Formal derivation makes an assumption operational.
- Vignettes make an emerging AI practice imaginable.
- Human/LLM comparisons make AI capability claims bounded and concrete.
- Deal arbitrage risk matching turns acquisition failure into a more credible
  empirical shock.

When polishing methods, connect the design to the theoretical mechanism before
listing measures.

### 5. Discussion Returns to the Starting Assumption

The discussion should not merely repeat findings. It should return to the
opening theoretical problem and explain how the paper changes the reader's
model of that problem.

Good discussion openings:

- "Traditional theory assumes X; our analysis shows when and how X can be
  repaired."
- "The evidence suggests that AI changes the process, but the strategic
  outcome depends on capability diffusion and firm-specific complements."
- "Prediction is useful in routine settings, but strategic uncertainty requires
  theory-guided intervention."
- "A failed growth move can trigger exit when the missing resource is a keystone
  complement."

## SS Wording and Claim Calibration

### Theory-Forward Concern Language

Use when opening or framing:

- "A long-standing assumption in..."
- "A central yet often implicit premise..."
- "This view leaves underspecified how..."
- "The prevailing account treats X and Y as separate..."
- "The core challenge is not whether X exists, but how it translates into Y."

### Mechanism Language

Use when building theory:

- "This mechanism operates because..."
- "The key insight is to..."
- "This creates an interdependence in which..."
- "The effect should be strongest when..."
- "If the proposed mechanism is correct, then..."
- "This perspective reveals X not merely as Y, but as Z."

### Evidence Language

Use when reporting:

- "We provide evidence consistent with..."
- "Our results indicate..."
- "Additional analyses suggest..."
- "The pattern is consistent with the proposed mechanism..."
- "We interpret this evidence as..."

Use "show" when backed by formal derivation, randomized experiment, or strong
identification. Use "suggest" or "is consistent with" for archival associations,
secondary analyses, and mechanism probes.

### AI/LLM Language

Use:

- "AI-augmented SDM", "LLM-generated evaluations", "LLM-generated strategies"
- "current LLMs", "off-the-shelf AI tools", "firm-specific AI use"
- "data-based prediction", "theory-based causal reasoning"
- "human-AI collaboration", "human-AI hybrid decision-making"

Avoid:

- "AI understands", "AI knows", "AI decides like a human"
- "AI will transform all strategy" unless framed as a scenario.
- "LLMs prove..." when the evidence is a task comparison or vignette.

### Contrarian Theory Language

Use a grant-and-narrow structure:

1. "We grant that X is powerful in settings characterized by..."
2. "However, this does not imply Y."
3. "The relevant distinction is..."
4. "Under uncertainty, the mechanism shifts from X to Y."

### Moderator Transition Language

Use the Gibbs pattern:

- "If this effect truly arises from [mechanism] rather than [alternative], it
  should be amplified when [mechanism condition] is more pronounced."

This is stronger than "we also test moderators" because it explains why each
moderator is diagnostic.

## Exemplar Matching Rules for Future Polishing

- For AI-optimistic but overclaimed manuscripts: use Csaszar et al. (2024) to
  keep claims capability-specific, process-based, and conditional on competitive
  dynamics.
- For AI-skeptical or theory-based-view manuscripts: use Felin and Holweg
  (2024) to build a contrarian argument without ignoring AI's strengths.
- For papers challenging an assumption or building a construct: use Csaszar and
  Eklund (2026) to make the operational method do the contribution work.
- For M&A/resource-reconfiguration papers: use Gibbs et al. (2026) to integrate
  literatures, define the mechanism, and make moderators diagnostic.
- For any SS introduction: ask which logic gap the manuscript repairs. If none
  is visible, the paper is not yet SS-ready.
