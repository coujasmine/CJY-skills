# Strategy Science Track Positioning

## Contents

- Major SS theoretical lineages
- Lineage diagnostic procedure
- Citation density expectations
- Lineage mis-fit remediation


SS does not have formal "departments" or "tracks" the way SMJ does, but it has clear theoretical lineages that recur across published papers. The skill uses these lineages to (a) verify fit, (b) calibrate which references and exemplars to cite, and (c) flag misalignments.

When the user's primary theoretical lineage is not one of these, the paper is at higher desk-reject risk regardless of empirical quality.

---

## Major SS theoretical lineages

### 1. Carnegie School / Behavioral Theory of the Firm

**Anchor citations**: Cyert & March 1963; Simon 1947, 1957, 1976, 1997; Newell & Simon 1972; Gavetti, Greve, Levinthal & Ocasio 2012; March 1991.

**Sub-themes SS publishes:**
- Bounded rationality and aspiration-based decision-making
- Search, exploration/exploitation
- Performance feedback and adaptive learning
- Organizational routines and adaptation
- Simulation/computational models of organizational learning

**SS exemplars**: Many. Qu et al. 2026 explicitly extends Cyert & March 1963 by arguing that predictions provide a forward-looking complement to backward-looking aspiration-based mechanisms. Kanis et al. 2026 anchors in the Carnegie tradition via Newell & Simon and Simon 1997.

**Fit signals**: References to aspiration levels, performance feedback, satisficing, search, problemistic vs. slack search.

### 2. Mental Representations / Cognitive Strategy / Strategic Foresight

**Anchor citations**: Csaszar 2018; Csaszar & Laureiro-Martínez 2018; Csaszar & Levinthal 2016; Csaszar & Ostler 2020; Csaszar et al. 2024a,b; Gavetti & Menon 2016; Gavetti & Levinthal 2000; Gavetti et al. 2005, 2007; Tripsas & Gavetti 2000; Barr et al. 1992; Kaplan 2008, 2011; Kapoor & Wilde 2023; Peterson & Wu 2021; Heshmati & Csaszar 2023.

**Sub-themes SS publishes:**
- Brunswik (1952) lens model applied to strategic cues
- Mental representations: breadth, depth, consensus (Csaszar & Laureiro-Martínez 2018)
- Cognitive flexibility, analogical reasoning, framing
- Strategic foresight as a managerial ability
- Updating beliefs in the face of new information

**SS exemplars**: Asghar et al. (foresight + knowledge breadth), Kanis et al. 2026 (mental representations under time constraints + LLM use), Qu et al. 2026 (Brunswik lens model + ML-derived predictions as forward-looking representations).

**Fit signals**: References to mental representations, cognitive maps, mental models, foresight, prediction errors, Brunswik lens, attentional engagement.

### 3. Strategic Human Capital

**Anchor citations**: Castanias & Helfat 2001; Helfat & Peteraf 2015; Helfat & Martin 2014; Custódio et al. 2013, 2019; Mannucci & Yong 2018; Lee & Csaszar 2020; Chen et al. 2021a; Crook et al. 2011; Campbell et al. 2012; Coff 1997.

**Sub-themes SS publishes:**
- Managerial knowledge breadth/depth and firm outcomes
- Generalist vs. specialist managers
- CEO/TMT human capital effects on firm strategy
- Career experience as a driver of cognitive structure

**SS exemplars**: Asghar et al. (career experience across industries/firms/functions → foresight). Mannucci & Yong, Lee & Csaszar, Chen et al. 2021a are heavily cited.

**Fit signals**: References to generalists/specialists, career experience entropy, BoardEx-style data, CEO/TMT characteristics.

### 4. Innovation Ecosystems / Platforms / Architecture

**Anchor citations**: Adner & Kapoor 2010; Adner 2017; Jacobides, Cennamo & Gawer 2018; Jacobides, Cennamo & Gawer 2024; Baldwin 2024; Baldwin & Woodard 2009; Hannah & Eisenhardt 2018; Kapoor 2018; Boudreau 2010; Boudreau & Hagiu 2009; Cusumano, Gawer & Yoffie 2019; Gawer 2014; McIntyre & Srinivasan 2017; Schmidt & Foss 2025; Wareham et al. 2014; Williamson 1985, 1991 (for architecture-environment fit).

**Sub-themes SS publishes:**
- Ecosystem architecture as a strategic choice
- Platform governance and complementor management
- Modular vs. integral architectures
- Coordination/coopetition in multi-firm settings
- Architecture-environment alignment

**SS exemplars**: Clough 2026 (governance trade-offs and ecosystem architectures), much of the platform/architecture literature.

**Fit signals**: References to platforms, complementors, modularity, hybrid governance, ecosystem participants, network effects.

### 5. Dynamic Capabilities / Microfoundations

**Anchor citations**: Teece, Pisano & Shuen 1997; Teece 2007; Helfat & Peteraf 2015; Eisenhardt & Martin 2000; Salvato 2009; Felin et al. 2012; Foss & Pedersen 2016.

**Sub-themes SS publishes:**
- Sensing/seizing/transforming capabilities
- Microfoundations of capability building
- Capability heterogeneity and competitive advantage

**Fit signals**: References to dynamic capabilities, microfoundations, sensing/seizing, capability deployment.

### 6. Transaction Cost Economics / Hybrid Governance / Institutional Economics

**Anchor citations**: Williamson 1985, 1991; Coase 1937; Langlois & Robertson 1995; Argyres & Bigelow 2010; Jacobides & Billinger 2006; Argyres et al. 2020; Zenger, Felin & Bigelow 2011; Schmidt & Foss 2025.

**Sub-themes SS publishes:**
- Make-vs-buy decisions
- Hybrid governance forms
- Vertical scope and integration
- Markets-and-hierarchies framework, modern extensions

**SS exemplars**: Clough 2026 integrates Williamson hybrid governance with game-theoretic coordination.

**Fit signals**: References to transaction costs, asset specificity, governance choice, make-or-buy, hybrid forms.

### 7. Formal / Game-Theoretic / Computational Strategy

**Anchor citations**: Schelling 1980; Farrell & Saloner 1988; Cooper et al. 1989, 1992; Van Huyck, Battalio & Beil 1990; Crawford 2019; Brandenburger 1996; Brandenburger & Nalebuff 1996; Simcoe & Watson 2019; Levinthal 1997; Rivkin 2000; Csaszar & Levinthal 2016.

**Sub-themes SS publishes:**
- Coordination games applied to strategy
- Pure formal-theoretic typologies
- Simulation-based theorizing (NK landscapes etc.)
- Cheap talk, signaling, equilibrium analysis

**SS exemplars**: Clough 2026 (game-theoretic coordination as building block for ecosystem architecture).

**Fit signals**: Payoff matrices, equilibrium analysis, NK simulations, cheap talk, mechanism design.

### 8. AI in Strategic Decision-Making (emerging)

**Candidate anchor citations to verify against the user's bibliography**: AI-strategy work such as Csaszar et al.; Doshi et al.; Raisch & Krakowski; Felin & Holweg; Boussioux et al.; Bouschery, Blazevic & Piller; Choudhary et al.; Agrawal, Gans & Goldfarb; and Shrestha, Ben-Menahem & von Krogh. Do not insert exact author-year claims unless the citation has been supplied or verified.

**Sub-themes SS publishes:**
- LLM use in strategy formulation and evaluation
- AI as cognitive augmentation vs. cognitive substitute
- Algorithmic decision-making in M&A, allocation, etc.
- Theory-based reasoning vs. ML-based prediction
- Predictions as a strategic construct (Qu et al. 2026)

**SS exemplars**: Kanis et al. 2026 and Qu et al. 2026 are in the recent "Can AI Do Strategy?" special issue (Vol. 11 No. 1, March 2026), alongside the Csaszar, Lee, Zemsky, and Zenger 2026 introduction and other AI-strategy articles. Verify any additional AI-strategy anchor before citing it.

**Fit signals**: References to LLMs, generative AI, prediction machines, AI-augmented decision-making, ML-derived measures.

### 9. Acquisitions and Corporate Strategy

**Anchor citations**: Haleblian & Finkelstein 1999; Kim & Finkelstein 2009; Capron & Pistre 2002; Schijven & Hitt 2012; Hayward 2002; Laamanen & Keil 2008; Bingham, Heimeriks & Meyer-Doyle 2024; Kumar et al. 2015; Welch et al. 2020; Pavićević & Keil 2021.

**Sub-themes SS publishes:**
- Acquisition decision-making, target selection, deal completion
- Post-merger integration, performance feedback
- M&A as a strategic-cognitive process

**SS exemplars**: Qu et al. 2026 (predictions in acquisition decision-making).

**Fit signals**: References to acquisitions, M&A, deal selection, post-deal performance, integration.

## Lineage diagnostic procedure

When the user provides a manuscript or abstract, identify the primary lineage:

1. **What is the dependent variable's nature?**
   - Cognitive/managerial decision → lineages 1, 2, 8
   - Firm-level capability or strategy → lineages 3, 5
   - Industry or ecosystem outcome → lineages 4, 6
   - Theoretical prediction or framework → lineages 7
   - Acquisition or corporate outcome → lineage 9

2. **What theoretical anchor citations appear in the introduction?**
   - Csaszar, Gavetti, Levinthal, Simon → lineages 1, 2
   - Castanias, Helfat, Custódio → lineage 3
   - Adner, Kapoor, Jacobides, Baldwin → lineage 4
   - Teece, Helfat-Peteraf → lineage 5
   - Williamson, Coase, Argyres → lineage 6
   - Schelling, Farrell-Saloner, Levinthal-Rivkin → lineage 7
   - Verified AI-strategy citations from the user's bibliography -> lineage 8
   - Haleblian-Finkelstein, Capron, Schijven-Hitt → lineage 9

3. **Cross-check**: does the lineage match the method?
   - Lineage 2 papers often use experiments (Kanis), archival with cognitive proxies (Asghar), or ML-derived representations (Qu).
   - Lineage 4 papers often use case studies, panel archival, or pure theory (Clough).
   - Lineage 7 papers are typically pure theory or simulation.
   - A lineage 1/2 paper using a single-source survey is a method-theory mismatch and at high reviewer risk.

4. **If the lineage is unclear or not on this list**: high desk-reject risk. The intro must do extra work to anchor in a recognized SS conversation. Common mis-fit lineages SS rarely publishes:
   - Pure social-psychology (without strategic decision implication)
   - Pure marketing-research
   - Pure finance-asset-pricing (unless framed via strategic decisions, as Qu does)
   - Pure HR-selection (unless framed as strategic human capital, as Asghar does)
   - International business country-comparison
   - Family business / SME governance (unless connected to a primary lineage)

## Citation density expectations

SS papers cite densely from their primary lineage. Norms (from the 4 exemplars):

- **Introduction**: 25-50 citations. Heavy density on the primary lineage in paragraphs 2-4.
- **Theory section**: 30-80 citations. Each subsection (2.1, 2.2, ...) cites 8-15 anchors.
- **Method section**: 10-25 citations (mostly for measurement and design choices).
- **Results section**: 5-15 citations (mostly for analytical method and prior findings comparisons).
- **Discussion section**: 15-35 citations (theoretical implications cite primary lineage; practical implications cite phenomenon literature).
- **Total references**: typically 80-200. Clough 2026 cites ~150; Asghar et al. cite ~110; Kanis et al. cite ~80.

If the user's manuscript has < 60 total references, the theoretical anchoring is likely thin for SS standards.

---

## Lineage mis-fit remediation

If the primary lineage does not match SS norms:

1. **Re-anchor**: identify the closest SS lineage and add 5-10 foundational references from that lineage in the introduction and theory section. This is not citation-padding; it is repositioning.
2. **Re-frame the RQ**: rewrite the research question in the vocabulary of the SS lineage (e.g., "how do mental representations shape strategic foresight" rather than "how do cognitive biases affect decision-making").
3. **Re-frame the contribution**: state the theoretical movement in terms a Csaszar/Gavetti/Levinthal/Adner-trained reader would recognize (mechanism extension, boundary refinement, integration).
4. **If re-anchoring is impossible**: the paper is mis-fit for SS. Retarget to SMJ, Organization Science, AMJ, or AMD. Do not force-fit.
